## What this means {#what-this-means}

Your store asked WCPOS to slow down for a moment.

Nothing to do — WCPOS waits and retries automatically. If this appears every day, ask your site admin about the store's rate limit.

## Your data {#your-data}

Don't clear this device's data — it may hold changes your store doesn't have yet. They go through on a later attempt.

## Troubleshoot {#troubleshoot}

1. Nothing to do — the POS backs off and retries by itself; retrying by hand only extends the throttle.
2. Nothing needs to be re-entered: the affected changes stay on the device and go through on a later attempt.
3. If this appears every day, ask the site administrator to find which layer returns the 429 — a security plugin, a CDN or firewall rule, or the host's own limits — and to allow the POS's REST traffic.
4. The expanded log entry's endpoint and timestamps help the administrator match the throttle in their own dashboards.

# Email Invoice Gateway

:::info Pro Feature
Email Invoice is a free plugin, but the POS offers it only with [WCPOS Pro](../../getting-started/free-vs-pro.mdx). Without Pro, the till takes Cash and Card.
:::

When a customer will pay later, send them an invoice instead of taking money at the till. They get your store's invoice email with a **Pay for this order** link. The order waits as **Pending payment** until they pay it online or come back to pay in the store.

It suits a business customer who pays through their accounts team, a large order that needs someone's approval, or a customer who leaves before paying.

Go straight to: [Send an invoice](#send) · [After you send it](#invoice-sent) · [Send it again or cancel it](#follow-up) · [Set it up](#set-up)

## Send an invoice {#send}

You need a connection, and the invoice covers the whole order: once any payment has been taken on it, Email Invoice can't be chosen.

1. Ring up the sale as usual. If the customer has an account, choose them as the cart's customer: their billing email fills in the address for you.
2. Press **Checkout**, then tap **Email Invoice** on the Payments tab.
3. Type the customer's email address, or check the one already filled in, then press **Send invoice**.

The store sends the email and marks the order **Pending payment**. Stock is taken off when the invoice is sent, the same as for a paid sale.

### The invoice form {#invoice-form}

Tapping **Email Invoice** puts this form above the keypad:

- **The note** — your gateway description. Out of the box it says the customer gets an email with a "Pay for this order" link.
- **Email address** — where the invoice goes. For a guest, type it. Until there is an address, the line under the amount says **Enter Email address** and the button stays greyed out.
- **Save email to billing address** — tick it to store this address as the order's billing email. Leave it clear to send the invoice without changing the order.
- **Send invoice · $45.00** — the button names the whole order total. An invoice is always for the whole order, so the keypad is locked.

## After you send it {#invoice-sent}

The checkout turns into the **Invoice sent** screen. Nothing was collected, so there is no tick and no change to give.

- **Invoice sent** — in place of the paid amount.
- **to jane@example.com · $45.00 due** — where it went and what the customer owes.
- **Receipt** — the order's receipt, ready to print, email or save as PDF, as on any sale. See [Receipts and the Paid Screen](../../receipts/at-checkout.mdx).
- **New sale** (**Print receipt & new sale** when receipts print automatically) — clears the till for the next customer. The order leaves the cart and waits on the [Orders](../../orders/index.mdx) screen.

### What the customer gets {#customer-email}

The customer gets WooCommerce's customer invoice email: the items, the totals and a **Pay for this order** link. The link opens your store's order payment page, where they pay with any payment method your online store offers. When they pay, the order turns paid in WooCommerce and on the Orders screen.

The email's look and wording come from `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Settings > Emails`.

## Send it again or cancel it {#follow-up}

On the **Orders** screen, open the order: tap its row, or with a mouse, the **›** at the end of the row. The order opens on its View tab.

- **Invoice sent to jane@example.com on Nov 7, 3:04 PM** — under **Payment**: where the invoice went and when.
- **Send again** — puts the order back in the cart. Press **Checkout**: **Email Invoice** is already chosen. Check the address and press **Send invoice**. The customer gets a fresh email for the same order, and only this newest invoice can be cancelled.
- **Cancel invoice** — the store drops the invoice and the order is open again, ready for another payment. It needs a connection.
- **Re-open** — puts the order back in the cart to change it or take payment. See [Re-opening Orders](../../orders/index.mdx#reopening-orders).

:::warning Cancelling doesn't unsend the email
**Cancel invoice** stops the till waiting for the customer. It does not take the email back. If the customer might still pay from it, tell them before you take another payment.
:::

### Take payment in the store instead {#pay-in-store}

The customer comes back to pay. They could still pay from the email too, so tell them you're taking payment now. Open the order on the Orders screen and press **Re-open**, then **Checkout**. Above the keypad, a line says **Invoice sent to jane@example.com on Nov 7, 3:04 PM**, with its own **Cancel invoice** button. Take the payment on any tile as usual: once money is taken, the invoice no longer waits for the customer.

## Set it up {#set-up}

Email Invoice uses your store's own email and online payment methods. It needs no account or keys.

1. **Install it.** In `WP Admin > POS > Settings > Extensions`, install **Email Invoice Gateway**. Or download the **plugin zip asset** (not the source-code zip) from the [GitHub releases page](https://github.com/wcpos/email-invoice-gateway/releases) and upload it in `Plugins > Add New > Upload Plugin`. Version 0.1.0 or later gets its own tile on the Payments tab; older versions show only on the [Legacy tab](../../pos/checkout/legacy-tab.mdx).
2. **Switch it on for the POS.** In `WP Admin > POS > Settings > Checkout`, switch on **Email Invoice Gateway** and save. See [Checkout Settings](../../settings/wp-admin/checkout.mdx#enabling-a-gateway). It never shows at your online checkout.
3. **Check the wording.** The tile's name and the note above the email field are the gateway's **Title** and **Description**, in `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Settings > Payments > Email Invoice Gateway`.

For the invoice to be any use, WordPress must be able to send email, and your online store needs at least one payment method the customer can use on the payment page.

<details>
<summary>For developers: how the form is drawn</summary>

The gateway declares its fields to the POS: a note, an email field filled from the order's billing email, a checkbox, and the **Send invoice** button label. The POS draws them with its own controls. A required field left empty shows **Enter** and the field's name; a list shows **Choose** and its name. Other gateways can declare fields the same way; see [Adding Payment Fields](../../reference/gateway-template.mdx#adding-payment-fields).

</details>

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

**Email Invoice isn't on the Payments tab.** It isn't switched on in Checkout Settings, or the extension is older than 0.1.0 and shows only on the [Legacy tab](../../pos/checkout/legacy-tab.mdx). Update it from `POS > Settings > Extensions`.

**"Email Invoice is not available right now".** Tap the line to see why. **Needs a connection**: an invoice can't be sent offline. **Takes the whole order, so not once a payment has been taken**: part of the order is already paid, so take the rest another way. See [Split Payments](../../pos/checkout/split-payments.mdx).

**"Please enter a valid email address".** The address has a typo. Fix it and press **Send invoice** again.

**The customer didn't get the email.** Ask them to check their spam folder, then press **Send again**. The order's notes in WooCommerce say whether the store sent it. If no invoice email arrives for anyone, the store's email setup needs a look: WordPress sends it, not the POS. An SMTP plugin or email service makes delivery more reliable.

**The customer can't pay from the link.** Your online store needs a payment method switched on for the web, in `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Settings > Payments`. Check too that the order hasn't been cancelled or paid since you sent it. Try the link yourself.

<details>
<summary>Messages when you cancel an invoice, or the till can't save it</summary>

**"The store did not cancel the invoice, so it still stands".** The customer may have paid it already. Check the order on the Orders screen before you take another payment.

**"A newer invoice has been sent for this order".** It was sent again, perhaps from another till, and this till hasn't caught up. Only the newest invoice can be cancelled: wait for the order to sync, then try again.

**"This invoice cannot be cancelled".** A payment has already been taken on the order, or no invoice is out any more. Check the order before you do anything else.

**"The invoice could not be cancelled. Try again".** The store didn't answer. Check the connection and press **Cancel invoice** again.

**"The invoice was sent, but this till could not save it"**, or **was cancelled at the store**. The store has the change; this till's copy catches up on the next sync. See [PAYMENT113](../../error-codes/PAYMENT113.mdx).

</details>

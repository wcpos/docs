Scan a barcode and the product goes straight into the cart. Almost every scanner works out of the box, because it types the code like a very fast keyboard and WCPOS tells a scan from typing by that speed. No scanner? Use the [camera](#camera-scanning).

## Scan a product {#scan}

Pair or plug in your scanner first ([Connect a scanner](#connection-modes)), or use the camera. If your store uses registers, [open the register](../register/index.mdx#open) first: while it's closed, nothing goes in a cart. Then put the cursor where your device needs it, and scan:

- **In a web browser, on any computer, tablet or phone, or in the desktop app**: click a blank part of the screen first. While a text field has the cursor, the search box included, WCPOS leaves the keys there as typing.
- **In the WCPOS app for iPad, iPhone or Android**: tap into the search box first. That's where a keyboard-mode scanner's keys arrive. A scanner you've [registered on Android](#android) works wherever the cursor is. {/* pending: monorepo#3368 */}

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={1} offsets={[[150, 0], [0, 0]]} alt="A scan has added a jacket to the cart: an empty search field and the new line in the cart" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Search** — empty again after the scan, on every device, ready for the next one.
2. **The new line** — the size and colour scanned. Scan the same code again to add another.

</div>

As it goes in, **Added to cart** with the product's name shows at the top for a moment, so you know what went in without looking at the cart.

In a browser or the desktop app, you can scan anywhere in the POS, the cart and checkout included.

A code shorter than **Barcode Minimum Length** (8 to start with) can't be added; see [Detection settings](#configuring-barcode-scanning).

:::note
While you're [counting the drawer](../register/index.mdx#close), scans are refused until the count is done.
:::

On the **Products** and **Orders** screens, a scan goes into that screen's search box instead, so you can scan a product to find it or its orders.

### What WCPOS looks for {#what-matches}

WCPOS first looks on this device for a product or variation whose barcode matches. If nothing does, it asks your store (**Searching store…**). When the store has it, the product is added, and the next scan of it is instant. Which field counts as the barcode is set in [Barcode Field](../../settings/wp-admin/general.mdx#barcode-field); a matching SKU, GTIN, UPC, EAN or ISBN works too.

<Accordion>
  <AccordionItem question="12-digit and 13-digit barcodes">

A 12-digit UPC-A code matches its 13-digit EAN form and back, so a package printed with 12 digits still finds a product saved with the leading zero.

  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem question="A product found in your store">

A product found in your store is downloaded to this device before it's added, so the next scan doesn't need the store. A new device fills in the rest of your catalogue on its own; see [Product Synchronisation](../../products/sync.mdx).

  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

A scan that can't add exactly one product tells you why, in a message at the top that stays for six seconds.

2. **The code** — left in the search field, so you can check what the scanner read. The list shows nothing until you clear it with the cross at the end of the field.

More than one product has this code. Pick the right one from the results, then give each product its own barcode.

The product matched but adding it failed. Scan again, or tap the product.

## Scan with the camera {#camera-scanning}

Every device can scan with its camera, so you don't need a scanner to start. Press the scan button beside the search field. The camera opens as a panel below the filters, so the product list stays in view.

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Scan** — opens the camera panel. Press it again to close it.
2. **Allow camera** — the first time, WCPOS asks for the camera. Press it, then allow the camera in the prompt that follows.

</div>

Hold a barcode in front of the camera. When it's read, the panel's border flashes green and the scan goes through exactly like one from a scanner, with the same [messages](#messages).

A note under the status warns you when a scan may not find everything:

- **Offline — only products already on this device will scan.**
- **Sync is still starting — some products may not be found yet.**
- **Local database unavailable — reload the app to restore scanning.**

## Scan sounds {#sounds}

A sound on every scan lets you keep your eyes on the customer. Turn on **Play a sound on scan** in **Settings** → **Barcode Scanning**, under **Sound**. It's off to start with, and it's saved on this device only, so each till can have its own.

  <AccordionItem question="Choose and test scan sounds">

<div style={{ maxWidth: '726px' }}>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={6} offsets={[[0, 0], [0, 0], [0, 0]]} alt="The Sound section of Barcode Scanning settings with Play a sound on scan turned on: three sound themes with preview buttons, Volume, Success sound and Failure sound" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **▶** — plays a theme's success sound.
2. **▶✕** — plays its failure sound.
3. **Volume ▶** — plays the success sound at the volume you've set.

</div>

</div>

- **Play a sound on scan** — the switch at the top. It turns sounds on and opens the options below it.
- **Sound theme** — **Classic** (bright rising blip / low buzz), **Checkout** (supermarket-style flat beep / double low tone) or **Soft** (gentle chime / muted knock, for quiet shops).
- **Volume** — drag the slider.
- **Success sound** — plays when a product goes into the cart.
- **Failure sound** — plays for every other message in [What the screen says](#messages). **Searching store…** is silent.
- **Failure vibration** — on iPhone, iPad and Android only. The device vibrates on a failure, with the failure sound or on its own if **Failure sound** is off. It needs **Play a sound on scan** on.

</AccordionItem>

## Detection settings {#configuring-barcode-scanning}

The **Scanning** section of the same screen decides what counts as a scan. Most stores never change it. If scans land as typing or short codes are refused, run **Barcode Scanning Test** there: it times your scanner and suggests values. See [Barcode settings](../../settings/store/barcode.mdx).

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={5} offsets={[[0, 0], [0, 0]]} alt="Settings, Barcode Scanning: the Scanning section with the threshold at 24, minimum length at 8 and empty prefix and suffix, above the Sound section" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Barcode Average Time Input Threshold (ms)** — how fast the keys must come to count as a scan.
2. **Barcode Minimum Length** — shorter codes are never added.

</div>

**Barcode Scanner Prefix** and **Barcode Scanner Suffix**, below them, remove characters your scanner adds around the code. Leave them empty unless your scanner is set to add characters.

<Accordion>
  <AccordionItem question="What each detection setting does">

| Setting | What it does | To start with |
|---|---|---|
| **Barcode Average Time Input Threshold (ms)** | When the average gap between keys is under this, the keys count as a scan. Slower keys are typing. | 24 |
| **Barcode Minimum Length** | Shorter codes are never added. Set it to your shortest barcode. | 8 |
| **Barcode Scanner Prefix** | Characters your scanner adds before the code, removed before the lookup. | Empty |
| **Barcode Scanner Suffix** | Characters your scanner adds after the code, removed before the lookup. | Empty |

**A code shorter than Barcode Minimum Length:** if it looks like a scan, all digits or ending with Enter, WCPOS shows **Barcode scanned** with **Barcode must be at least 8 characters long**, at most once every 30 seconds. A short burst of letters is taken for fast typing and left alone.

  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Connect a scanner {#connection-modes}

Almost every scanner arrives in keyboard mode, and in keyboard mode it needs no setup: pair or plug it in and scan. That is right for most stores. For a guided path through setup or troubleshooting, use the [Scanner Setup Wizard](../../hardware/scanners/setup-wizard.mdx).

A direct connection is worth it if scans sometimes arrive garbled, such as capitals and symbols mixed up by a keyboard layout, or if you want scans to reach WCPOS wherever the cursor is. A directly connected scanner is recognised by the device itself, not by typing speed, keyboard layout or focus.

<Accordion>
  <AccordionItem question="Connect a scanner directly (advanced)">

Every barcode scanner ships in one of a few modes, and the mode decides how WCPOS receives its scans:

| Mode | How it arrives | Setup |
|---|---|---|
| **Keyboard (HID)**, the factory default on almost every scanner | The scanner types the code; WCPOS recognises the fast keys | None: pair or plug it in and scan |
| **HID-POS** | A direct USB connection | Switch the scanner's mode, then **Connect over USB** |
| **Serial (SPP / USB-COM)** | A direct serial connection over Bluetooth or USB | Switch the scanner's mode, then **Connect over Bluetooth serial**. A Bluetooth scanner that isn't listed may use its own service UUID: add it under **My scanner isn't in the list**. |
| **Bluetooth LE** | A direct Bluetooth Low Energy connection, in the iPhone and iPad app | Remove the scanner from your device's Bluetooth settings and don't pair it again, then press **Connect Bluetooth scanner**. It connects to the first Bluetooth LE scanner it finds, so keep any other one switched off. |

The connect buttons are in **Settings** → **Barcode Scanning** → **Scanner** → **Connect a scanner directly (advanced)**.

:::info A scanner in keyboard mode can't be connected directly
Your device treats a keyboard-mode scanner exactly like a keyboard, and no app is allowed to take over a keyboard. If your scanner doesn't appear in the list after you press a connect button, it is almost certainly still in keyboard mode.
:::

**To switch modes**, find the setup barcode in your scanner's manual (look for *"SPP mode"*, *"serial mode"* or *"HID-POS mode"*) and scan it. The scanner reconfigures itself. For a Bluetooth scanner, forget the old pairing in your device's Bluetooth settings and pair again after switching. Scanning the manual's *"HID"* or *"keyboard"* barcode switches it back at any time.

USB and Bluetooth serial connections work in the desktop app and Chrome-based browsers. The desktop app shows a device chooser inside the app; Chrome shows its own. The iPhone and iPad app connects to a Bluetooth LE scanner directly. Safari and Firefox have no direct connection: use keyboard mode or the camera.

  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

### Register a scanner on Android {#android}

Android can't connect to a scanner directly, but it can recognise a keyboard-mode scanner by the device it comes from. A registered scanner's scans are caught before they reach any text field, with no typing-speed guesswork, so they work wherever the cursor is. **Barcode Minimum Length** still applies.

1. Pair or plug in the scanner.
2. In **Settings** → **Barcode Scanning**, in the **Scanner** section, tap **Register a scanner**, then **Register scanner**.
3. Scan any barcode with that scanner. WCPOS shows **Detected "…" — name it and save**.
4. Name it and tap **Save scanner**.

The scanner gets its own row marked **Registered**. Other keyboard-mode scanners still work without being registered. See [Register your scanner on Android](../../hardware/scanners/setup-wizard.mdx).

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

- **Nothing happens when you scan** — in a web browser or the desktop app, click a blank part of the screen and scan again. In the WCPOS app for iPad, iPhone or Android, tap into the search box first. Then check the scanner in **Barcode Scanning Test**; see [Barcode settings](../../settings/store/barcode.mdx).
- **The code lands in the search box as typing** — in a web browser or the desktop app, the cursor was in the field: click a blank part of the screen and scan again. In the WCPOS app for iPad, iPhone or Android, keep the cursor in the search box. Either way, the scanner may be slower than **Barcode Average Time Input Threshold (ms)**: run the test and press **Apply** on its suggestion.
- **Barcode not found for a product you know** — check the [Barcode Field](../../settings/wp-admin/general.mdx#barcode-field) setting and the product's barcode in WooCommerce. A product marked online only never appears in the POS. On a new device, wait for the first sync to finish: **Store health → Database** shows its progress.
- **Scanning unavailable** — you're offline and the product isn't on this device yet. Check [Store health](../../support/store-health.mdx#database).
- **The camera won't start** — see [the camera messages](#camera-scanning).

## F.A.Q. {#faq}

<Accordion>
  <AccordionItem question="A product I just added to my store won't scan. Why?">

WCPOS asks your store for any code it doesn't have on this device, so a new product scans as long as you're online. If you get **Barcode not found**, the store doesn't have that code either: check the product's barcode field in WooCommerce. If you get **Scanning unavailable**, you're offline; scan it again once you're back online.

  </AccordionItem>

  <AccordionItem question="Does the POS generate and print barcodes?">

No, not at this time. WCPOS scans and reads existing barcodes, but it doesn't create or print them.
To generate barcodes for your products, use a WooCommerce plugin that specialises in barcode creation and printing, for example:

- [EAN for WooCommerce](https://wordpress.org/plugins/ean-for-woocommerce/)
- [A4 Barcode Generator](https://wordpress.org/plugins/a4-barcode-generator/)

Then add each code to the product's barcode field, and it scans at the till.

  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

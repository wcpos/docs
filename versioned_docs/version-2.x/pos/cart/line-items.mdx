Every product, fee and shipping charge in the cart is a line. You change most of a line right where it sits: tap the quantity, the price or the name. Everything else is in the line's **Edit** dialog.

## A line in the cart {#line}

A product line shows its quantity, its name and its unit price, with the line total on the right. Fees and shipping are lines too, below the products. The [columns](./index.mdx#columns) you show decide what else is there.

<div style={{ maxWidth: 379 }}>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={1} offsets={[[0, 32], [122, 0], [0, -26], [0, -26], [-34, 0], [-34, 0], [-30, 0], [0, 24]]} alt="The cart with three lines: 3 × Aim Analog Watch at $45.00 with the custom field Engraving: Happy birthday, a Gift wrapping fee of $2.00 and Local delivery shipping of $5.00" />

</div>

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Qty** — the quantity. Tap it to change it; see [Change the quantity](#quantity).
2. **Item** — the product's name. Tap it to rename the line for this sale: the name turns into a text box, and Enter or tapping anywhere else keeps the new name. Underneath are the options a [variation](../product-panel/variations.mdx) was sold with and the line's [custom fields](#meta-data), as `Key: value`. Turn on the column's **SKU** option to show the SKU too.
3. **Price** — the price of one. Tap it to change it; see [Change the price](#price). When the price is lower than the regular price, the regular price shows struck through above it.
4. **Total** — quantity times price. Tap it to show **Edit** and **Remove**; see [Edit or remove a line](#remove-item).
5. **A fee** — tap its name to rename it, the same way, and its amount to change it. See [Fees](./index.mdx#fees).
6. **A shipping line** — the same: tap its title or its amount. See [Shipping](./index.mdx#shipping).
7. **+** — adds a fee, shipping, a discount or a [miscellaneous product](./index.mdx#miscellaneous-products) to the cart.
8. **Cart Settings** — which side the cart sits on, how it sorts its lines and which columns it shows; see [Cart Settings for lines](#cart-settings).

</div>

## Change the quantity {#quantity}

Tap the quantity box. In the iOS and Android apps you type the number straight into the box, and a quantity of 0 removes the line. Everywhere else a keypad opens: type the new quantity, then tap the tick (or press Enter) to keep it. On a phone the keypad opens as a sheet from the bottom of the screen; on a tablet or computer it grows from the box, with the quantity at the top:

<div style={{ maxWidth: 441 }}>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={2} offsets={[[0, -24], [0, -24], [0, -24], [-6, 0], [-6, 0], [0, 0]]} alt="The quantity keypad open over a line of 1: a bin on the left of the 1, a plus on the right, the number keys, a delete key and a tick" />

</div>

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **The quantity** — what you've typed so far.
2. **−** — one fewer. At 1 it turns into a bin, as here: pressing it removes the line.
3. **+** — one more.
4. **Number keys** — type the new quantity.
5. **Delete** — takes off the last digit.
6. **Done** (the tick) — keeps the new quantity. Enter does the same. Escape closes the keypad and keeps the quantity you had.

</div>

When your store has [Enable Decimal Quantities](../../settings/wp-admin/general.mdx#enable-decimal-quantities) on, the keypad also has a decimal key, marked with your store's decimal separator. It takes a part quantity such as 2.5 or 0.25 (up to six decimal places), for things sold by weight or length.

When your store prevents overselling, a quantity above what you have in stock turns amber; see [When checkout is blocked](#checkout-blocked).

### Split a line {#splitting-line-items}

**Split** turns one line of several into separate lines of one each, so you can give one of them a different price, discount or note. A part quantity keeps its fraction on a line of its own: a line of 2.5 splits into 1, 1 and 0.5.

The **Split** link sits under the quantity of any line with more than one. It's off until you switch it on in **Cart Settings**.

## Change the price {#price}

Tap the price, type the new price for one (or press a quick discount key), then tap the tick (or press Enter) to keep it. The price grows into a keypad:

<div style={{ maxWidth: 293 }}>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={3} offsets={[[-30, 0], [-4, -20], [-4, -20], [-4, -20], [0, 0], [-4, 0], [0, 0]]} alt="The price keypad on a $45.00 line with the −10% key lit: $45.00 −10 % above the new price $40.50, the number keys, a decimal point, a delete key and a tick" />

</div>

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **The price** — the new price for one. After a quick discount the sum shows above it: here $45.00 −10 %, making $40.50.
2. **−5%** — a **quick discount** key: it takes 5% off the line's price.
3. **−10%** — lit, because it's been pressed. Press a lit key again to undo it.
4. **−15%** — takes 15% off. These three are where you start; you can [change them](./discounts.mdx#quick-discounts).
5. **Number keys** — type the new price for one. The first digit replaces the old price.
6. **Delete** — takes off the last digit.
7. **Done** (the tick) — keeps the new price. Enter does the same. Escape closes the keypad and leaves the price as it was.

</div>

On a phone the same keypad opens as a sheet.

:::note A price edit is not a WooCommerce "discount"
A lowered price is recorded as the item's sale price. Receipts can show the saving, but the order's **Discount** total is reserved for coupons, and reports count the sale at the price paid. See [Cart Discounts](./discounts.mdx#why-tracked-differently) for why WooCommerce treats the two differently.
:::

## Edit or remove a line {#remove-item}

Each line has **Edit** and **Remove** buttons hidden under it. To show them:

- **Tap the line's total.** Tap it again to hide them.
- **Hover** over the total with a mouse and the line slides aside to give you a peek.
- **Swipe the line** to the left on a touch screen, or drag it left with the mouse.

<div style={{ maxWidth: 379 }}>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={4} offsets={[[6, -28], [6, -28]]} alt="The watch line slid to the left, showing a blue Edit button and a red Remove button" />

</div>

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Edit** — opens the [Edit dialog](#edit-form).
2. **Remove** — takes the line out of the cart.

</div>

Swipe further and **Remove** fills the whole row in red: let go and the line is removed in one move.

The message that appears after a removal has **Undo**, which brings the line back as it was.

## The Edit dialog {#edit-form}

**Edit** opens the line's details in a dialog named after it. Changes save as you make them; press **Close** when you're done.

For a product line:

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={5} offsets={[[-35, 0], [-35, 0], [-35, 0], [-35, 0], [-35, 0], [-10, 0], [-10, 0], [-35, 0]]} alt="Edit Aim Analog Watch: Name, Quantity 3, Price 45.00, Tax Standard rate, SKU 24-MG04 with Remove, the custom field Engraving: Happy birthday, the Regular Price and Custom field buttons, and Close" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Name** — what the receipt and order call it.
2. **Quantity** — the same as in the cart.
3. **Price** — the same as in the cart. When the line is on sale, the regular price shows beside it with **was**. Its **Remove** drops the regular price: the line is no longer on sale, and the price stays as it is.
4. **Tax** — whether the line is taxable, and its tax class.
5. **SKU** — shown when the product has one. A line without a SKU has a **SKU** button to add one.
6. **Regular Price** — adds a regular price to a line that isn't on sale.
7. **Custom field** — adds a key and a value, like the **Engraving** row above it; see [Custom fields](#meta-data).
8. **Close** — closes the dialog. Everything you changed is already saved.

</div>

Each added row has its own **Remove**. A [miscellaneous product](./index.mdx#miscellaneous-products) isn't in your catalogue, so its dialog also has **Categories**, **Virtual** and **Downloadable**.

### A fee line {#edit-fee}

**Edit** on a fee opens the fee's own dialog, named after it. It saves as you go, like a product's.

A fee can't be negative: the dialog says **A fee cannot be negative. Use Add Discount instead.** See [Discounts](./discounts.mdx).

### The JSON tab {#raw-json-view}

The **JSON** tab beside **Form** shows the line exactly as it goes to WooCommerce. You won't need it for a sale. It helps when you're checking what a plugin will see, or why a field isn't saving.

#### The three kinds of line {#line-item-types}

WooCommerce stores products, fees and shipping as separate kinds of line:

| Kind | In WooCommerce | Examples |
|------|----------------|----------|
| Product | `line_item` | T-shirt, Coffee mug |
| Fee | `fee_line` | Gift wrapping, Service charge |
| Shipping | `shipping_line` | Standard shipping, Express delivery |

#### WCPOS's own fields {#protected-keys}

WooCommerce calls custom fields meta data. Keys that start with an underscore are WCPOS's own records (such as `_woocommerce_pos_data` and `_reduced_stock`). They stay out of the form and the cart, and you can see them on the **JSON** tab.

</details>

## Custom fields {#meta-data}

A custom field is a note kept with one line, such as an engraving or a serial number. To add an engraving, open the line's **Edit** dialog, tap **Custom field**, type **Engraving** in **Key** and the customer's message in **Value**. The field shows under the line's name in the cart, and on the order in WP Admin. Each one has its own **Remove**.

Some fields come with the product: any [Meta Data Keys](../product-panel/meta-data-keys.mdx) you've chosen are copied onto the line when you add it.

If the form says **This key is managed by WCPOS. Choose another key.**, you've typed one of [WCPOS's own keys](#protected-keys). Pick a different key.

**Cart Settings**, the sliders icon above the cart, sets which side the cart sits on and the order of its product lines. Fees and shipping always stay last, in the order you added them.

## When checkout is blocked {#checkout-blocked}

When your store [prevents overselling](../../settings/wp-admin/checkout.mdx#prevent-overselling), the cart checks every product line against your stock:

- A notice above the lines lists each one that's short: **Only 2 available for Hoodie**, with **Reduce**, which opens that line's quantity, or **Hoodie out of stock**, with **Remove**.
- The short lines are marked in amber with what's available.
- **Checkout** dims. Pressing it takes you to the first line to fix.

Fix every line and **Checkout** comes back. A product on backorder doesn't block the sale.

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

**"Couldn't update that item — it's no longer in the cart."** The line was removed while you were changing it. Check the cart and make the change again.

**"That item is no longer in the cart."** You pressed **Remove** on a line that had already gone. Nothing else is needed.

**There's no Split link.** Turn on **Split** under the **Qty** column in **Cart Settings**. The link only shows on lines with a quantity above 1.

**Checkout is dimmed.** A line is short of stock. See [When checkout is blocked](#checkout-blocked).

**A custom field won't save.** Its key starts with an underscore and belongs to WCPOS. Choose another key.

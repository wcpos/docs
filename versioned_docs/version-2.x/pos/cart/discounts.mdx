There are two places to take money off a sale. Tap an item's price to lower that one item. Choose **Add Discount** in the add menu to take money off the whole order, as an amount, a percentage or a coupon.

:::info Looking for the Discount total?
A price lowered at the till is recorded as the item's sale price, not as a WooCommerce discount. Only coupons, and **Add Discount**, which is recorded as a coupon, count in the **Discount** total. [Why price changes and coupons are tracked differently](#why-tracked-differently) explains why.
:::

## Take money off one item {#quick-discounts}

Tap the price on a line. A keypad opens under it, with your quick discount keys along the top.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={1} offsets={[[-120, 0], [-88, 0], [-88, -24], [65, 0], [-24, 0]]} alt="The price keypad open on a $45.00 line with −10% pressed: the new price $40.50 with $45.00 −10% above it, the three quick discount keys, the digit keys, the delete key and the done key" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **New price** — what the item will cost, and how it was worked out from the price before.
2. **Quick discount keys** — each takes its percentage off the line's price. Pressed the wrong one? Press another before **Done**; it replaces the first. You choose the keys; see [Set your quick discount keys](#quick-discount-keys).
3. **Digits** — type a new price instead.
4. **Delete** — removes the last digit.
5. **Done** — sets the new price. Tapping outside the keypad does the same. Press **Escape** to close it and keep the old price.

</div>

When you press **Done**, the line total changes to the quantity × the new price.

A key always works from the price the line had when you opened the keypad. On a $45.00 item, **−10%** shows $40.50; press **−5%** instead and it shows $42.75. Press **−10%** again and **Done**, and $40.50 is now the line's price. Open the keypad again and press **−10%**: $40.50 becomes $36.45.

### Type a new price {#line-item-discounts}

For a price match, a staff price or any other one-off, type the price the customer pays and press **Done**. A key on a keyboard works too: type the price and press **Enter**.

Each line is priced on its own. To take 10% off three different items, do it on each line, or take it off the whole order with [Add Discount](#order-level-discounts). See [Line Items](line-items.mdx#price) for the rest of what you can change on a line.

### Set your quick discount keys {#quick-discount-keys}

The keys are set on each device. Press the sliders button at the top of the cart to open **Cart Settings**. **Quick Discounts** shows the keys as the cashier sees them on the price keypad. A new device starts with 5%, 10% and 15%.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={2} offsets={[[0, 0], [0, 0], [0, 0]]} alt="Cart Settings with the Quick Discounts row: three keys showing 5%, 10% and 15%, drawn as they appear on the price keypad" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **First key** — tap it and type a percentage from 1 to 100.
2. **Second key** — the same.
3. **Third key** — the same. You can have up to three keys.

</div>

To take a key away, type 0 on it. The remaining keys stretch to fill the row. With no keys at all, the price keypad shows only the digits. There are no fixed-amount keys; for an amount off, type the new price or use [Add Discount](#order-level-discounts).

### Different prices on the same product {#split}

A customer buys five of something and gets two at a lower price. Turn on **Split** for the **Qty** column in [Cart Settings](index.mdx#columns). A line with more than one item then shows **Split** under its quantity.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={3} offsets={[[0, 36], [12, 54], [12, 54]]} alt="A cart line of three Aim Analog Watches at $45.00, total $135.00, with Split under the quantity" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Split** — turns the line into one line per item.
2. **Price** — each new line keeps this price until you change it.
3. **Line total** — all three together. After **Split**, each new line shows its own.

</div>

Press **Split** and the line becomes one line for each item, every one at the same price. Lower the price on the lines you want to discount. A quantity such as 2.5 splits into two lines of one and a line of 0.5. See [Splitting Line Items](line-items.mdx#splitting-line-items).

## Take money off the whole order {#order-level-discounts}

Press **+** at the top of the cart and choose **Add Discount**. The dialog opens on **Quick discount**.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={4} offsets={[[0, 0], [0, 0], [0, 0], [12, 46], [50, -52]]} alt="The Add Discount dialog on the Quick discount tab: the Quick discount and Coupon tabs, the amount 10, the dollar and percent switch set to percent, and the Add Discount button" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Quick discount** — an amount or a percentage off this order.
2. **Coupon** — one of your store's coupons instead. See [Apply a coupon](#coupon).
3. **Amount** — tap it and type on the keypad.
4. **$ or %** — a fixed amount off, or a percentage of the order.
5. **Add Discount** — puts the discount on the order.

</div>

The discount appears with the order's totals, with the line totals adjusted to match.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={5} offsets={[[12, 46], [100, 0], [50, 0], [0, 0]]} alt="The cart after a 10% discount on a $45.00 watch: the line total $45.00 struck through with $40.50 under it, the subtotal $45.00, a Discount (10%) row taking off $4.50 with its remove button, and Checkout $40.50" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Line total** — the price before the discount, struck through, and what the customer pays now.
2. **Subtotal** — the order before the discount.
3. **Discount (10%)** — the discount and how much it takes off. **Discount** alone means a fixed amount. Press **✕** to remove it.
4. **Checkout** — the new total.

</div>

A fixed amount is shared across the items in proportion to their prices. A percentage comes off every item. You can add more than one discount to an order; each appears on its own row.

**Add Discount** works offline. It is recorded on the order as a coupon line, so it counts in WooCommerce's **Discount** total and tax is worked out on the lower price. It doesn't create a coupon in your store.

### Remove a discount {#remove-discount}

Press **✕** on its row in the totals. The line totals go back, and a message says **Discount removed**. Prices you changed on lines stay as you set them.

## Apply a coupon {#coupon}

In **Add Discount**, switch to **Coupon**. This works on the free plugin too.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={6} offsets={[[-40, 0], [10, 46], [24, -52]]} alt="The Add Discount dialog on the Coupon tab: the Select coupon field, the New coupon button beside it, and the Apply button" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Select coupon** — type part of the code to find it.
2. **New coupon** (WCPOS Pro) — make a coupon now. See [Create a coupon at the till](#new-coupon).
3. **Apply** — checks the coupon's rules and puts it on the order.

</div>

A coupon that can't be used on this order says why. For what each message means, removing a coupon, and coupons offline, see [Applying Coupons](../../coupons/applying-coupons.mdx).

## Create a coupon at the till {#new-coupon}

:::info Pro Feature
Making a coupon at the till needs [WCPOS Pro](/getting-started/pro-license). On the free plugin, **New coupon** says **Upgrade to Pro**.
:::

1. Press **New coupon** on the **Coupon** tab.
2. Type a code, or press **Generate**.
3. Choose the discount type and type how much it takes off.
4. Set **Expires**, and any other rules the coupon needs.
5. Press **Add Coupon**. The coupon is saved to your store first, then applied to this sale, and a message says **… saved and applied to this sale**.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={7} offsets={[[-40, 0], [22, -40], [-40, 0], [-60, 0], [50, -52]]} alt="The Add Coupon form with the code SPRING10 typed: the Generate link beside the code, the discount type set to Percentage, the expiry choices, the One-time use switch, the buttons for more coupon rules, and the Add Coupon button" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Code** — the code customers will use. It's the only field you must fill in.
2. **Generate** — makes up a code for you.
3. **Discount** — a percentage, an amount off the order, or an amount off the products, and how much.
4. **One-time use** — the coupon stops working after it's used once.
5. **Add Coupon** — saves the coupon to your store, then applies it to this sale.

</div>

**Expires** sets how long the coupon lasts. The buttons below the form add more of WooCommerce's coupon rules, such as **Minimum spend**, **Total uses** or **Cannot be combined**.

The coupon is a real WooCommerce coupon, so it's there for the next sale, in **WP Admin → Marketing → Coupons**, and in reports. To make coupons from the Coupons screen instead, see [Creating coupons](../../coupons/index.mdx#creating-coupons).

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

- **A fee cannot be negative. Use Add Discount instead.** — the POS doesn't take money off with a minus fee. Use [Add Discount](#order-level-discounts). For fees themselves, including **Amount Includes Tax**, see [Fees](index.mdx#fees).
- **Enter an amount greater than zero** or **Enter a percentage above 0 and up to 100** — the **Add Discount** amount is empty or out of range.
- **… is already on the order, and it can't be combined with other coupons.** — a coupon marked **Individual use only** is on the order. Remove it first, or leave the discount off.
- **New coupon is greyed out** — its tooltip says why: **Upgrade to Pro**, no permission (an administrator grants it in **WP Admin → POS → Settings → Access**), or **Coupons are created when the store is reachable**. You can still apply coupons your store already has.
- **… saved but not applied** — the coupon is in your store, but its rules don't fit this order. Check its rules, or pick it again from **Select coupon** once the order fits.

## POS Discounts vs WooCommerce Coupons {#pos-discounts-vs-woocommerce-coupons}

**A line price** (a quick key or a typed price) — one line, set by the cashier. It's recorded as the line's price, like a sale price, so it isn't in the WooCommerce **Discount** total. No rules. Best for a price match, a staff price or a damaged item.

**Add Discount** — the whole order, set by the cashier. It's recorded as a coupon line, so it counts in the **Discount** total. No rules of its own, but it can't join a coupon marked **Individual use only**. Best for a one-off amount or percentage off.

**A WooCommerce coupon** — whatever its rules allow: usage limits, products, minimum spend, expiry, customer email. Set up in **WP Admin → Marketing → Coupons**, or at the till with Pro. It counts in the **Discount** total. Best for promotions you want to limit and report on.

## Why price changes and coupons are tracked differently {#why-tracked-differently}

A quick discount key or a typed price is recorded the way WooCommerce records a sale price: the item is sold at the price the cashier set. So the **Discount** total stays at zero, coupons work from the lowered price, and coupons with **Exclude sale items** skip items whose price was lowered. The full background, including how the prices are stored, is in the [POS Discount Reference](../../reference/pos-discounts.mdx).

<details>
<summary>How WooCommerce records coupons and sale prices</summary>

WooCommerce has exactly one built-in idea of a discount: the **coupon**. A coupon belongs to the order. It's recorded in its own field, counted in the **Discount** column of reports, and carries its own rules such as usage limits and expiry dates.

A **sale price** is not a discount to WooCommerce. It's simply the product's price right now: when an item on sale is sold, the order records the price paid and nothing else. Reports count it as ordinary revenue.

Receipts are the one place customers expect to see what they saved, so WCPOS records the regular price alongside the new price on each line. A receipt can then show sale savings and coupon discounts as separate figures without counting either twice, and without changing WooCommerce's totals or reports.

</details>

## How POS Price Changes Interact with Coupons {#how-pos-price-changes-interact-with-coupons}

Say a cashier lowers an item from $18 to $16, then applies a coupon. The coupon works from **$16**, not $18, so the customer isn't discounted twice against the original price.

- An item whose price was lowered at the till counts as on sale, the same as a WooCommerce sale price. A price raised at the till doesn't. A coupon with **Exclude sale items** skips items on sale, and a fixed cart coupon with **Exclude sale items** can't be used at all while one is in the cart.
- Removing a coupon leaves each line at the price set at the till.

:::note Developer Reference
**Add Discount** lines are coupon lines named `pos-discount`, then `pos-discount-2`, and so on. The `wcpos_item_is_on_sale` filter changes what counts as on sale. For how till prices are stored and the other filters, see the [POS Discount Reference](/reference/pos-discounts).
:::

## Orders with a negative fee {#discount-fee-tax}

Before 2.0, a cashier could take money off an order with a negative fee. The POS no longer allows that, but older orders can still carry one.

<details>
<summary>How WCPOS taxes a negative fee, and how WooCommerce differs</summary>

WCPOS taxes a negative fee from **the fee's own tax settings**. With its tax status set to **None**, no tax is applied to it. Left **Taxable**, it's taxed at its own tax class. The amount on the fee is the amount taken off: a fee of `-10.00` means the customer pays 10 less.

WooCommerce on its own does this differently. It spreads a negative fee's tax across every other item in the order, ignoring the fee's tax status. On a store where **Prices Entered With Tax** is *Yes*, it would also take **12.00** off for a `-10.00` fee at a 20% rate. WCPOS keeps the amount the cashier entered.

This belongs to the order, not to the app you open it in: recalculating a POS order from **WP Admin → Orders** keeps it. Orders that didn't come from the POS keep WooCommerce's behaviour.

:::note Tax on a negative fee
With the fee's tax status set to **None**, the customer pays the right total but the **tax recorded on the order doesn't go down**, so it's slightly higher than the lower total warrants. It errs in the tax authority's favour, never the other way. A taxable negative fee takes tax off at its own tax class's rate. Discounts made with **Add Discount** are coupon lines, so tax is worked out on the lower price.
:::

</details>

## What changed in v1.9.0 {#what-changed-in-v190}

If you upgraded from v1.8 and the **Discount** total on receipts and reports now shows **0**, this is why. Earlier versions recorded a till price change as a WooCommerce discount, which broke coupon math: a coupon applied on top worked from the original price, so the customer was undercharged. From v1.9.0, the price set at the till **is** the line price, exactly as WooCommerce records a product on sale, and only coupons count as discounts. See [Why price changes and coupons are tracked differently](#why-tracked-differently).

### What this means for you {#what-this-means-for-you}

<details>
<summary>Receipts, reports, coupons and stored prices since v1.9.0</summary>

- **Receipts** can show the regular price, the saving against it, and a combined **Total saved** for those savings plus coupons. Price savings stay separate from the WooCommerce **Discount** field.
- **Reports** show `discount_total = 0` when only line prices were changed. Only coupons are counted, and **Add Discount** counts as a coupon.
- **Coupons** calculate correctly on items whose price was lowered at the till.
- **The regular and selling prices are stored** in the line's POS metadata (`_woocommerce_pos_data`), so a receipt can show the saving without changing WooCommerce's discount totals.

</details>

### Showing the original price and savings on receipts {#showing-the-original-price-and-saving-on-receipts}

The bundled templates that show prices include the regular price and the saving when a product is on sale or its price was changed at the till. They also show **Total saved** when WCPOS can work out a complete figure for the order. That total combines regular-price savings with coupon discounts without counting either twice.

<details>
<summary>Update a receipt template you made before v1.9.0</summary>

Templates copied from the gallery are your own copies, and WCPOS updates don't overwrite them. If you made your receipt before this change, either:

1. click **Use Template** on a fresh copy of the bundled template; or
2. add the fields from the [Receipt Data Reference](/receipts/receipt-data#displaying-regular-price-and-savings) to your template.

The fields for savings:

| Field | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `lines[].regular_price_display` | Recorded regular unit price |
| `lines[].selling_price_display` | Selling unit price before coupons |
| `lines[].unit_savings_display` | Saving per item |
| `lines[].line_regular_total_display` | Regular-price total for the line |
| `lines[].line_savings_display` | Total saving for the line |
| `totals.sale_savings_total_display` | Total regular-to-selling-price savings from catalogue sales and till price changes |
| `totals.discount_total_display` | WooCommerce discount total: coupons, including **Add Discount** |
| `totals.total_saved_display` | Combined regular-price savings and WooCommerce discounts |
| `totals.total_saved_complete` | Whether the combined total is complete and safe to display |

Most of these fields are only there when the order has the data behind them. Custom templates should guard each one, and hide the **Total saved** row when the figure is incomplete or nothing was saved. The [Receipt Data Reference](/receipts/receipt-data#totals) has the pattern.

WooCommerce reports are unchanged: their discount totals hold coupons, not sale-price or till-price savings. Use `totals.total_saved` for receipts, and use a coupon or **Add Discount** when a saving must also show as a WooCommerce discount in reports.

</details>

## Known Limitations {#known-limitations}

- **No automatic discount rules.** The POS doesn't do "buy 2, get 1 free" on its own. Use WooCommerce coupons for structured promotions.
- **Quick discount keys are percentages only.** For an amount off, type the new price, or use **Add Discount** with **$**.
- **Negative fees on older orders.** With tax status **None**, the order total is right but the recorded tax doesn't go down. See [Orders with a negative fee](#discount-fee-tax).

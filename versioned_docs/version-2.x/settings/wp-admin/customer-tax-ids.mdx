A customer buying for a business often needs their tax ID on the receipt: a VAT number, an ABN, a CPF, a GSTIN. Add it to the customer once, and the POS copies it onto each of their orders and prints it on the receipt. You can also add one to a single sale.

Most stores need no setup. The **Tax IDs** section in **WP Admin > POS > Settings > General** only decides which WordPress field the numbers are saved in, so that a VAT plugin you already use keeps working. See [Choose where tax IDs are saved](#plugins-and-field-names).

## Add a tax ID to a customer {#add}

:::info With WCPOS Pro
Adding and editing customers in the POS needs Pro. On the free plugin, add a tax ID [to the sale](#one-sale) instead.
:::

1. Open the customer form: in the cart, tap **+** and choose **Add new customer**, or press **+** on the **Customers** screen. For a customer you already have, press the open arrow (›) on their row in **Customers**.
2. Press **Tax ID**. A row opens.
3. Choose the **Type**, type the **ID** and check the **Country**. See [The Tax ID row](#row).
4. Press **Add Customer**. When you edit an existing customer there's no button to press: each change saves as you make it. The rest of the form is on [Customers](../../customers/index.mdx#add-new-customer).

### The Tax ID row {#row}

The row is the same on the customer form and in the cart's **Edit Customer Address**, shown here.

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Tax ID** — adds a row. Press it again for a second number, such as an Italian customer's Codice Fiscale and Partita IVA.
2. **Type** — which kind of number this is. A new row starts on the first type for the customer's billing country, or your store's country while the billing address is empty, and on **Other** when no type fits that country. See [Tax ID types](#supported-types).
3. **ID** — the number. The empty field shows an example of the right shape for the type, and a long number scrolls inside the box. When it saves, spaces are taken out and letters become capitals.
4. **Country** — two letters. Choosing a type fills it in: **GB** for **UK VAT**, **BR** for **Brazil CPF**. For **EU VAT** and **Other** it takes the billing country, so check it for an EU customer from another member state.
5. **Remove** — takes the row off.

</div>

The **Type** list puts the types for the country first, above a line, then every other type. An EU VAT number is typed with its country letters, as **DE123456789**. If you type it without them, the store adds the **Country** in front when it saves, for **EU VAT** and **UK VAT** only.

## Add a tax ID to one sale {#one-sale}

In the cart, open the order's address and press **Tax ID**. The row works the same way as on the customer form. The number is saved on this order only, as you type, and prints on its receipt. This works on the free plugin too.

To keep the number on the customer's profile as well, press **Update customer too**. That button shows for a customer with an account, and only to a cashier who may edit customers. See [Edit the order's address](../../pos/cart/index.mdx#address).

## What the order keeps {#tax-ids-are-copied-onto-orders}

When you choose a customer for a sale, the POS copies their tax IDs onto the order. They show under [the order's address](../../pos/cart/index.mdx#address), where you can change them for this sale. The order keeps the numbers it was sold with:

- Changing a customer's tax ID later doesn't change their past orders, so an old receipt reprints as it was.
- A tax ID you change or add in the cart is used for that order instead of the customer's.
- Changing an order's tax IDs changes that order only. Nothing copies them from the customer again.
- A guest sale has no tax ID unless you add one to the sale.

## On the receipt {#on-receipts}

The **Standard Receipt**, **Detailed Receipt**, **Invoice** and **Quote** templates, and the detailed thermal receipts, print the customer's tax IDs with their details. Each number has its own label, such as **VAT ID**, **ABN** or **CPF**: see the table under [Tax ID types](#supported-types). To print them in your own template, see [Tax ID objects](../../receipts/receipt-data.mdx#tax-id-objects).

## Find a customer by tax ID {#finding-customers-by-tax-id}

Type the number into the customer search, in the cart or on the **Customers** screen. Search also finds numbers a VAT plugin saved before you used WCPOS, from the plugins listed under [Choose where tax IDs are saved](#plugins-and-field-names). See [Customer Search](../../customers/index.mdx#customer-search).

## Choose where tax IDs are saved {#plugins-and-field-names}

WordPress keeps a customer's tax ID in a named field (a meta key), and VAT plugins each use their own name for it. WCPOS saves into the field your store already uses, so a number typed at the till shows up in your VAT plugin, and a number your plugin saved shows up in the POS. Nothing needs moving.

Open **WP Admin > POS > Settings > General** and scroll to **Tax IDs**. The banner says what WCPOS found:

- **Auto-detected** — names the plugin it found, as "… is installed." WCPOS saves VAT-shaped tax IDs to the field that plugin reads. No action needed from you.
- **No plugin detected** — WCPOS uses its built-in field names, or the field your recent orders already use.

<details>
<summary>How WCPOS picks the field, and the plugins it detects</summary>

For each type, WCPOS takes the first of these that applies:

1. Your own field name, if you set one under [Use a different field name](#choosing-the-field-name-yourself).
2. The field of a plugin it detected.
3. With no plugin detected, the field used most on your last 200 orders.
4. Its built-in field name.

The plugins it detects:

- WooCommerce EU VAT Number
- Aelia EU VAT Assistant, including whether Aelia found the number valid
- WPFactory EU/UK VAT Manager
- WooCommerce Germanized, and Germanized Pro
- Brazilian Market on WooCommerce, and Extra Checkout Fields for Brazil
- NIF/CIF Spain for WooCommerce

The built-in field names:

| Type | Field name |
|---|---|
| EU VAT, UK VAT, Saudi Arabia VAT, Australia ABN, Canada GST/HST, US EIN, Other | `_billing_vat_number` |
| Brazil CPF | `_billing_cpf` |
| Brazil CNPJ | `_billing_cnpj` |
| India GSTIN | `_billing_gstin` |
| Italy Codice Fiscale | `_billing_cf` |
| Italy P. IVA | `_billing_piva` |
| Spain NIF/CIF | `_billing_nif` |
| Argentina CUIT | `_billing_cuit` |

</details>

### Use a different field name {#choosing-the-field-name-yourself}

Only do this if your plugin keeps tax IDs under a name WCPOS doesn't know. In the **Tax IDs** section, open **Advanced: use a different field name**. It shows one row per type:

- **Type** — the kind of tax ID.
- **Field name in use** — where WCPOS saves that type now.
- **Source** — **Plugin** (from a detected plugin), **Built-in** (WCPOS default) or **Custom** (your override).
- **Override** — type the field name your plugin uses. The box suggests the names WCPOS knows from other plugins. It saves when you choose a name; there's no Save button.

To go back to the automatic name, clear the **Override** box: it reads "(leave blank to use default)". The banner counts the overrides you have set.

:::note
Only customer tax IDs appear here. Your own business's numbers, such as a USt-IdNr., KVK or SIRET number, are set under [Store Tax IDs](./store-tax-ids.mdx).
:::

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

<div style={{ maxWidth: '560px', marginInline: 'auto' }}>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={3} alt="A Tax ID row with Type EU VAT and ID DE1234, and under the ID the warning Format does not match the selected type." />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Format does not match the selected type.** — the number doesn't have the shape of the type you chose.

</div>

</div>

- **Format does not match the selected type.** — check the **Type** first, then the number with the customer: a digit missing, or an EU VAT number without its two country letters. It's a warning only, and the number still saves. The POS checks the shape of the number, never whether a tax office has issued it.
- **An old receipt shows the old number** — the order keeps the number it was sold with. Change the tax ID on that order, not on the customer.
- **No tax ID on the receipt** — the sale was to a guest, or the customer had none when it was sold. Add one to the sale before payment, and check that your receipt template is one that shows customer details.
- **Your VAT plugin doesn't show numbers typed at the till** — in **WP Admin > POS > Settings > General > Tax IDs**, open **Advanced: use a different field name** and compare **Field name in use** with the field your plugin reads. Set an **Override** if they differ.
- **Update customer too isn't there** — the sale is to a guest, or your WordPress role can't edit customers. The tax ID is still saved on the order.
- **Add new customer is greyed out** — it needs Pro, and a role that can create customers.

**WP admin:** `WP Admin → POS → Settings → Sessions`.

**Sessions** shows who is signed in to the POS and on which devices, so you can sign out a lost tablet or a cashier who has left without touching the device.

Administrators and Shop Managers can use it. A Shop Manager sees everyone's sessions except an administrator's.

<details>
<summary>What counts as a session</summary>

Each time someone signs in to the POS, the store keeps a session for that device: one per sign-in in the desktop app, and usually one per browser for the POS in a browser.

</details>

## Find a person's devices {#find}

The screen has two columns.

- **People on the left.** Everyone with at least one session you may manage. When you're signed in to the POS yourself, you come first with a **You** chip; the rest follow by most recent activity. The number on each row is how many sessions that person has, and a green dot means **Active now**. Type in **Filter users** to search by name or username.
- **Their sessions on the right.** Select a person to see their name, their username and how many sessions they have, the **Logout All** button, and one card per device, the most recent first. [Read a session card](#card) explains what each card shows.

<JourneyShot manifest={wpAdmin} device="desktop" step={1} offsets={[[130, 0], [180, 0], [-36, 0], [-32, 0]]} alt="WP Admin, POS → Settings → Sessions: Maya Chen, Leo Park and Priya Nair on the left with their session counts, and Maya Chen selected on the right with Logout All and one card each for her desktop app and her web browser">

1. **Filter users** — search the list by name or username.
2. **The selected person** — the number is how many sessions they have.
3. **Logout All** — signs every one of this person's devices out.
4. **Terminate** — signs out just the device on that card.

</JourneyShot>

## Sign a device out {#signing-devices-out}

:::warning Ending a session doesn't change the password
Anyone who knows the password can sign straight back in.

- **A cashier who has left:** also remove their POS access in [Access](./access.mdx), or change their password.
- **A lost or stolen device:** change the password of each person who was signed in on it.
:::

### Sign out one device {#one-device}

1. Check with the person that the till isn't in use. No green dot doesn't mean it's idle: a till in use can go 30 minutes without one.
2. Select the person on the left.
3. Find the device by its **Application**, **IP** and **Created** time.
4. Press **Terminate** on its card. **Terminate session?** asks you to confirm. Press **Terminate**.

**Session terminated successfully** confirms it, and the session leaves the list.

### Sign out all of a person's devices {#all-devices}

1. Check with the person that none of their tills is in use. No green dot doesn't mean they're idle: a till in use can go 30 minutes without one.
2. Select the person on the left.
3. Press **Logout All**. **Terminate all sessions?** asks you to confirm. Press **Logout All**.

**Sessions terminated successfully** confirms it, and their sessions leave the list.

<details>
<summary>What a password change does</summary>

It ends every POS session the person has, on every device, at once, and stops anyone signing back in with the old password. They sign in again with the new one on their other devices.

</details>

### What happens on the device {#on-the-device}

The device's sign-in is cancelled straight away. The next time the POS connects to your store, it opens the WordPress sign-in window and shows **Session expired**: the same as when a sign-in runs out. See [AUTH101](../../error-codes/AUTH101.mdx).

Nothing on the device is wiped. Sales that haven't reached the store wait on the device and send once someone signs in again. On the Connect screen the person's row shows **Sign in again**. See [Connect to your store](../../getting-started/connect.mdx#multiple-users-per-store).

Ending your own session from here signs your POS out, not WP Admin.

## Read a session card {#card}

<JourneyShot manifest={wpAdmin} device="desktop" step={2} offsets={[[188, 0], [72, -4], [72, -4], [-28, 0], [80, 0], [8, 22]]} maxWidth={560} alt="One session card: Desktop Application 1.8.6 on macOS, its Last active time, its IP address, its Created and Expires dates, the User Agent toggle and the Terminate button">

1. **Application** — the app and its version, such as Desktop Application or Web Application. Below it, the browser and the operating system.
2. **Last active** — when the device last signed in or renewed its sign-in, as _Just now_ or _3 hours ago_. A POS in use renews about every 30 minutes.
3. **IP** — the internet address it signed in from, or _N/A_.
4. **Created** — when the person signed in on this device.
5. **User Agent** — press it to see the raw text the device sent. Support may ask for it.
6. **Expires** — when the sign-in runs out and the person has to sign in again: 30 days after **Created**. Using the POS doesn't push it back.

</JourneyShot>

**Application**, **IP** and **Created** together tell one device from another.

**Active now:** the green dot means the person signed in or renewed a sign-in in the last 5 minutes.

## Other ways a session ends {#other-ways}

You don't have to clear sessions by hand. A session also ends when:

- it reaches **Expires**, 30 days after the sign-in;
- the person's password is changed or reset: every POS session they have ends;
- the person signs out of WordPress in a browser: the POS session in that browser ends, and their other devices stay signed in.

<details>
<summary>For developers: session length and the session limit</summary>

The 30 days come from the `wcpos_jwt_refresh_token_expire` filter, and the 30-minute renewal from `wcpos_jwt_access_token_expire`. See [Token Expiry Coverage](../../reference/role-endpoint-access.mdx#token-expiry-coverage).

A person can hold up to 200 sessions. When a new sign-in would go over that, the store removes the sessions that have been idle longest, and only ones not seen for a week. A device in use is never removed this way.

</details>

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

**No active sessions found.** Nobody you can manage is signed in to the POS.

**A person you expect isn't listed.** They have no session that hasn't expired, or they are an administrator and you're signed in as a Shop Manager.

**The device signed straight back in.** Someone there still knows the password. Change it, or remove the person's POS access in [Access](./access.mdx).

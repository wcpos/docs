## What this means {#what-this-means}

The payment gateway the cashier chose on the tender pane refused the payment, or ended without taking it. A gateway refuses in one of three ways: it answers with a failure and its own message, it hits an error while it runs, or it tries to send the customer to another site, which the POS can't follow.

The gateway's own message, or a line naming the other site, is in the message at the till and on the log row.

A gateway that only rejects what was typed, such as an email address it won't accept, shows its message on the payment form instead, under that field or below the fields, with no error code.

## Your data {#your-data}

The POS recorded no payment. The order is unchanged, with its previous payment method put back, and its balance is still owed.

If the gateway hit an error after it had started, the store adds an order note asking you to check with the payment provider whether money was taken.

## Troubleshoot {#troubleshoot}

### At the till {#at-the-till}

Do this before the customer leaves.

1. Read the gateway's message on screen or on the log row.
2. Correct the details (the email address, the option chosen) and send again.
3. If the gateway keeps refusing, take a different payment method and tell the store owner.

### On the store {#on-the-store}

For the site administrator.

1. Check the gateway's settings on the WooCommerce payments page; a gateway that refuses every attempt is usually misconfigured or missing credentials.
2. Look under *WooCommerce > Status > Logs*. The WCPOS log, source `woocommerce-pos`, records a gateway error or a redirect to another site; many gateways keep their own log there too.
3. Check the order's notes for a note from the store about the payment attempt.

## Find the entry in the log {#log-entry}

Every code is logged in **Store health → Logs**. Press **Errors**, then tap the entry with this code to open it under its row. The picture shows an example entry, AUTH201; on your till the entry shows this page's code.

1. **Errors** — the filter that's on.
2. **The entry** — the code in **Status** is its error code.
3. **What happened and what to do** — what your store said, when it said something, then one line on what to do. When it matters, a warning comes first, such as **Payment has been taken — don't charge again.** or **Don't clear this device's data**.
4. **Help** — opens the code's page in these docs.
5. **Open record** — opens the order, product or customer the entry is about, when this till has it, your store has WCPOS Pro, and you're allowed to edit it.
6. **Copy** — copies the whole entry for support.

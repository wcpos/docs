**Symptom:** after you press **Connect**, red text under it ends "ask your host about the Authorization header.", with a **Learn more** link to this page. A pop-up with a **Help** button says "Ask your host to let the Authorization header reach WordPress."

**Do this now:** send whoever looks after your website a link to this page, since nothing at the till can get past the block.

## Fix it at the till {#at-the-till}

1. Send whoever looks after your website a link to this page.
2. Leave the address as it is. Your store answered the POS, so the address is right.
3. Once they've fixed it, press **Connect** again.
4. **If it connects:** press **Sign in with WordPress** and carry on.
5. **If the red text shows again:** the block is still there. Tell them again.

## Is anything lost? {#your-data}

No. Nothing on this device or on your store changed. The app stopped before it saved your store.

## If it keeps happening {#keeps-happening}

Your store's server, or a firewall, proxy or security plugin in front of it, strips the sign-in from every request. Only whoever looks after your website can change that.

<details>
<summary>For whoever looks after your website</summary>

The app's test request reached WordPress, but neither the `Authorization` header nor the `authorization` URL parameter arrived. There is no third way to send the sign-in, so this is fixed on the server.

1. Let the `Authorization` header through to WordPress. On Apache: `SetEnvIf Authorization "(.*)" HTTP_AUTHORIZATION=$1`.
2. Keep WordPress's generated `RewriteRule` that passes `HTTP_AUTHORIZATION`. Security plugins sometimes remove it.
3. Behind a proxy or CDN, forward the `Authorization` request header to the origin.
4. If a firewall (WAF) filters URL parameters, allow `authorization` on `/wcpos/` REST routes.

</details>

## Still stuck? {#still-stuck}

Contact support with the red text and your store's address. Logs can't be opened from this screen.

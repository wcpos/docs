**Symptom:** after you press **Connect**, a pop-up reads "Another authentication plugin is preventing WCPOS from connecting." with a **Help** button. Under **Connect**, red text gives the reason, with a **Learn more** link to this page.

**Do this now:** send whoever looks after your website a link to this page, since nothing at the till can let WCPOS through.

## Fix it at the till {#at-the-till}

1. Copy down the red text under **Connect**. It is your website's own message, or the app's when your website sends none.
2. Send it, with a link to this page, to whoever looks after your website.
3. Once they've changed the plugin, press **Connect** again.
4. **If it connects:** press **Sign in with WordPress** and carry on.
5. **If the red text shows again:** the plugin still blocks WCPOS. Tell them again.

## Is anything lost? {#your-data}

No. Nothing on this device or on your store changed. Your store turned WCPOS away before anything was saved.

## If it keeps happening {#keeps-happening}

A security or login plugin on your website shuts out visitors who aren't signed in, and WCPOS reads your store before anyone signs in. Only whoever looks after your website can change that.

<details>
<summary>For whoever looks after your website</summary>

The app reads your store's REST API index (`/wp-json/`) before anyone signs in. It answered 401 or 403 with a JSON error, the kind a plugin such as Force Login sends.

1. Find the plugin whose message the cashier sent you.
2. In that plugin's settings, let visitors who aren't signed in reach the REST API, or exempt the WCPOS routes. See [Plugin Conflicts](../support/troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx#security-and-login-plugins).
3. If the message names no plugin, switch security and login plugins off one at a time on a staging copy.
4. Then the cashier presses **Connect** again.

</details>

## Still stuck? {#still-stuck}

Contact support with the red text and your store's address. Logs can't be opened from this screen.

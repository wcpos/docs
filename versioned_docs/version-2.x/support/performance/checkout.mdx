Checkout is slow when your store is slow to answer the till. A sale waits for your store twice: when you press **Checkout**, the order is saved to your store, and when you press **Take**, your store records the payment before the receipt shows. This page shows where the till waits, how to measure your store, and what to change.

:::note Cashiers: measure, then hand over
The fixes are made on your website, mostly in WP Admin. If you don't run the website, [time a test sale](#time-a-sale) and send the times, with a link to this page, to whoever does.
:::

## Time a test sale {#time-a-sale}

Time a test sale before and after each change to see what it buys you. Each count is time the till spends waiting for your store:

1. Count from **Checkout** until **Take** stops reading **Saving order…**.
2. Pay, and count the wait for the way you paid:
   - **Cash, Card or another payment recorded at the till** — from **Take** until the receipt shows.
   - **A card reader** — from tapping its tile until it reaches **On terminal**, then again from **Approved** until the receipt shows. Leave out the time the customer spends at the reader.
   - **The Legacy tab** — from opening the tab until **Process Payment** can be pressed.
3. Note the **typical response** from [Store health → Performance](#store-health).

Repeat step 2 for each way you take payment, and change one thing at a time between tests. [Where the till waits](#where-it-waits) explains what each count measures.

## Where the till waits for your store {#where-it-waits}

**Checkout** — the order is saved to your store. The checkout opens at once, but the keypad doesn't respond and **Take** reads **Saving order…** until your store answers. After 4 seconds a message says the store isn't answering: see [what to do](#store-not-answering).

**Take**, on Cash, Card or another payment recorded at the till — your store records the payment, and then the receipt shows. If your store doesn't answer, the till asks again after 1, 3 and 9 seconds. If it still has no answer: see [what to do](#payment-not-answered).

**A card reader tile** — your store creates the payment, then confirms what the reader did. The tile then reaches the **On terminal** step and reads **Waiting for the customer on the terminal**, or **Present card on the reader** on a reader connected straight to this till. If the till keeps failing to reach your store while it waits, it reads **Connection unstable — still waiting**. See [Taking a Payment](../../pos/checkout/taking-a-payment.mdx#card-reader).

**The Legacy tab** — your store sends its payment page. A loading placeholder shows, and **Process Payment** stays greyed out until the page loads. If it doesn't load: see [what to do](#payment-form-didnt-load). See [The Legacy Tab](../../pos/checkout/legacy-tab.mdx).

Each wait is a request to your store, so a store that answers slowly makes every sale slow. Offline, a payment recorded at the till doesn't wait: the sale is held on this device and sent when the store is back. See [Offline Mode](../../getting-started/offline.mdx).

## Measure your store {#measure}

A test sale times one sale. **Store health** shows how your store has answered this till all day.

## Speed up saving and paying {#saving}

Check these possible causes one at a time, and [time another test sale](#time-a-sale) after each change.

### Order storage {#order-storage}

If WooCommerce still keeps orders in WordPress posts (legacy storage), every save at checkout gets slower as your store grows. Check which storage you use and switch to HPOS: see [Order Storage (HPOS)](./order-storage.mdx).

### Plugins that run when an order is saved {#2-plugin-interference-during-order-creation}

Some plugins do their own work each time an order is saved or paid, and the till waits for them. An accounting plugin that sends the order to Xero, for example, holds every sale until Xero answers. Turning plugins off one at a time on a copy of your website finds the one.

<details>
<summary>Find the plugin, step by step</summary>

Plugins that often do work on every order:

- Accounting and bookkeeping (Xero, QuickBooks)
- Email marketing tools that act on new orders
- Inventory and stock systems
- Shipping calculators
- Analytics and tracking

To find the one:

1. On a [staging site](../troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx#before-you-start-use-a-staging-site), turn off every plugin except WooCommerce and WCPOS.
2. [Time a test sale](#time-a-sale).
3. If it's much faster, turn the plugins back on one at a time, timing a sale after each, until it slows down again.
4. Ask that plugin's developer whether its work can run in the background instead.

The full method is in [Plugin Conflicts](../troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx#step-2-the-plugin-elimination-method).

</details>

### Your hosting {#hosting}

If the **typical response** is slow as well as your checkout times, your server may be short of resources. Take the times from [your test sale](#time-a-sale) to your host, with the **Server load** figure, so they can look into it. See [Server Performance](./server.mdx) for recommended specifications.

<details>
<summary>What to ask your host</summary>

- CPU and memory use while you sell
- The PHP memory limit and the number of PHP workers
- Slow database queries, and old data that could be cleaned up
- Object caching (test it with the POS before you rely on it)
- A larger plan, or a VPS instead of shared hosting

</details>

## Speed up the Legacy tab's payment page {#legacy-page}

Only gateways on the **Legacy** tab load a web page at checkout. It's a stripped-down copy of the WooCommerce page customers use to pay for an order online, sent by your website each time. Your theme's header, footer and layout are already gone, but the styles and scripts your theme and plugins add to every page still load, and they can make the page slow or break it. Changing what it loads is a job for a shop manager. If the page doesn't load at the till, see [what to do](#payment-form-didnt-load).

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={3} offsets={[[28, 34], [28, 34], [337, -273], [-30, 0], [-30, 0]]} alt="The Legacy tab at checkout: the payment page from the store with the Checkout Settings button at its top right, and the Process Payment button below it" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Payments** — tiles recorded at the till. They load no page.
2. **Legacy** — gateways that run on your store's own payment page.
3. **Checkout Settings** — opens the form for choosing which styles and scripts this page loads. The page around it is sent by your website.
4. **Process Payment** — greyed out until the page has loaded.
5. **✕** — back to the cart, with the order kept.

</div>

To remove styles and scripts from this page:

1. Sign in to the POS as a shop manager or administrator. A cashier can open the form but can't save it.
2. Press **Checkout**, open the **Legacy** tab, and press **Checkout Settings** on the payment page.
3. Untick a style or script to remove it. Start with your theme's styles, then scripts your gateway doesn't need.
4. Press **Submit**, then try a test payment with each Legacy gateway you use.

<details>
<summary>What the Checkout Settings form shows</summary>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={4} offsets={[[28, 34], [-28, 0]]} alt="The Checkout Settings form open on the Legacy tab's payment page: Disable wp_head and Disable wp_footer at the top, then the styles list and the scripts list side by side, with greyed entries that are always removed" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Legacy** — the tab the form belongs to.
2. **Checkout Settings** — the form, over the payment page. Every style and script on the page has a checkbox, ticked if it loads.

</div>

The form has three parts, from the top:

- **Disable All Styles and Scripts** — **Disable wp_head** and **Disable wp_footer** remove everything the page's header or footer would load, including what the payment form needs. Use them only to test whether styles and scripts are the cause.
- **Disable Selected Styles**, on the left — one entry per style. Your theme's styles are named after it, such as `twentytwentyfive-style`.
- **Disable Selected Scripts**, on the right — one entry per script.

Greyed entries ending *always removed on the POS payment page* are removed already and can't be ticked.

</details>

:::warning Keep what your gateway needs
If a payment method stops working after you remove a script, tick it again and press **Submit**.
:::

<details>
<summary>What WCPOS removes on its own, and options for developers</summary>

The payment page always drops the admin bar, the extra links WordPress adds to a page's header, and these WooCommerce styles and scripts:

- Styles: `admin-bar`, `woocommerce-general`, `woocommerce-inline`, `woocommerce-layout`, `woocommerce-smallscreen`, `woocommerce-blocktheme`, `wp-block-library`
- Scripts: `admin-bar`, `wc-add-to-cart`
- With WooCommerce Stripe older than 11.1.1, also its `wc-stripe-upe-classic` script and styles and `stripelink_styles`.

For developers:

- Saving the form needs the `manage_woocommerce` capability.
- The filters `wcpos_payment_template_dequeue_style_handles` and `wcpos_payment_template_dequeue_script_handles` add handles to the removed lists.
- The template can be overridden by copying it to `yourtheme/woocommerce-pos/payment.php`. The original is [templates/payment.php](https://github.com/wcpos/woocommerce-pos/blob/main/templates/payment.php) in the plugin.
- Only styles and scripts added with `wp_enqueue_style` and `wp_enqueue_script` can be removed. A theme or plugin that prints its code straight into the page can't be; ask its developer to enqueue it, or remove it with that plugin's own hooks.

</details>

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

### The store isn't answering {#store-not-answering}

*"The store isn't answering. Keep waiting, or go back to the cart."* Your store is taking more than 4 seconds to save the order. Keep waiting, or press **✕** to go back to the cart with the order kept. If you see it often, [time a test sale](#time-a-sale) and check the causes under [Speed up saving and paying](#saving).

### The store has not answered the last payment {#payment-not-answered}

*"The store has not answered the last payment. Try again to confirm it; it will not be taken twice."* Press **Try again**.

### The payment form didn't load {#payment-form-didnt-load}

*"The payment form didn't load — check the connection to your store, then retry."* The Legacy tab waited 90 seconds for the payment page, reloaded it once, and gave up. Check the connection and press **Retry**. If the page is slow every time, see [Speed up the Legacy tab's payment page](#legacy-page).

### You don't have permission to modify checkout settings {#no-permission}

*"You do not have permission to modify checkout settings."* You pressed **Submit** in **Checkout Settings** as a cashier. Sign in as a shop manager or administrator.

### Still slow {#still-slow}

If checkout keeps taking more than 10 seconds after all this, ask on [Discord](https://wcpos.com/discord). Press **Copy debug info** in **Store health → Logs** and paste it in, with the times from [your test sale](#time-a-sale) and what you've tried. See [Help & Troubleshooting](../index.mdx).

:::info With WCPOS Pro
Gateways on the Legacy tab need [WCPOS Pro](../../getting-started/pro-license.mdx). The free version takes Cash and Card.
:::

Every till talks to your WordPress site all day: it checks for changes, sends sales and fetches what a cashier looks up. How fast your server answers sets how fast the POS feels. When your server struggles, each till slows its own checks down and recovers by itself. This page shows where to see that happening, what it means and what to change on your hosting.

Here because of something you saw?

- [**Easing off** or **Paused until …**](#pace) under **Your server, over time**. You can keep selling: only background checks slow down, so changes from other tills may take longer to appear. If it shows most days, [choose a lighter preset](#fix) first.
- [A **503**, **429** or **414** error](#503-errors)
- [Searches and sales are slow on every till](#fix)

## See how your server is coping {#store-health}

Open **Store health** from the pulse icon at the foot of the menu, then **Performance**. The figures are for this till only.

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Store health** — the pulse icon at the foot of the menu.
2. **Performance** — how often this till checks your store, and how your server is coping.
3. **Last 24 hours** — requests, data and **typical response** in milliseconds. If this number keeps rising, include it in [what you send your host](#when-to-seek-help).
4. **Engine uptime** — one cell per hour. Amber means at least one request failed that hour. Hover over or tap a cell for its counts.
5. **Eco** — the gentlest preset. Choose it on tills that don't need to see other tills' sales quickly. See [Sync presets](../store-health.mdx#sync-presets).
6. **Right now** — when the next check is due, and about how many requests an hour this till makes at its settings.

## Read "Your server, over time" {#server-load}

Scroll down the same tab. **Your server, over time** says what pace the till is keeping and charts the last 24 hours.

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Right now** — the next check and the hourly request estimate, as above.
2. **Pace** — here **Normal pace**, with your server reporting high load. When the till slows down, it says why. See [What the pace line means](#pace).
3. **Server load** — the busiest load your server reported in each hour. The figure beside the title is the latest hour's. It counts everything running on your server, not just the POS.
4. **POS requests** — how many requests this till made in each hour.

<AccordionItem question="If the charts are empty">

Both charts read **Not enough data yet — this trend fills in as the till runs** until the till has readings from two different hours, as in this shot. Leave the till running and look again later.

Read the two charts together. If load rises with the POS requests, the tills may be part of it. If load rises on its own, something else on your server is busy: the website, a backup, another site on the same host. Either way, a screenshot of both charts is what your host needs to see. If the **typical response** is slow while the load stays low, your server isn't short of power: look at order storage and your plugins first (steps 2 and 3 of [When your server keeps struggling](#fix)).

### What the pace line means {#pace}

The line reads one of these:

- **Normal pace** — the till checks at your setting.
- **Normal pace — your server reports elevated load** (or **high load**) — your server says it's busy, and the till is still at your setting. Nothing to do unless it shows every day.
- **Easing off — checking N× less often: …** — the till checks less often, and names the reason after the colon. See [What each reason means](#reasons) below.
- **Paused until …** — your server named a time to come back, and the till waits until then. You can keep selling while it waits.

The line changes colour while the till is easing off or paused. Both clear by themselves once your server recovers. If they show most days, see [When your server keeps struggling](#fix).

:::note Only background checks slow down
Only the till's regular check for changes, and its background checks, slow down. A search, a barcode lookup or a sale goes to your server straight away. While it eases off, this till takes longer to see changes made on other tills and in WooCommerce.
:::

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="What each reason means, by how much the till slows, and how it recovers">

<a id="reasons"></a>

- **your server asked the POS to slow down** — your server, or a firewall in front of it, answered "too many requests" (**429**) once.
- **your server returned errors** or **requests to your server timed out** — three server errors (**5xx**) or timeouts within a minute. The reason names the last of the three.
- **your server was answering slowly** — the typical (median) answer time over the last ten answers was more than two seconds.
- **your server reported high load** — most of the last ten answers said your server is under high load while the till was at its normal pace, or two load readings in a row were at least double your server's usual load, once the load is 1 or more.

Each time, it checks half as often as before. It never goes slower than once every 5 minutes, or twice your setting when that's longer: every 10 minutes on **Eco**. A server that names its own pause (a **Retry-After** header) gets it, up to 15 minutes.

Recovery is slower on purpose: ten good answers, at least a minute after the last slowdown, halve the wait again. A load jump is the exception: the till checks half as often, never slower, until two load readings in a row are back within a quarter of your server's usual load. A till that restarts goes back to your setting.

WCPOS itself asks for short pauses: 5 seconds while the plugin is being updated on your site, 30 seconds while its sync tables are being installed. You may see **Paused until …** for a moment then.

For the engine rules behind this, see [Sync Performance](../../reference/sync-performance.mdx#server-pressure).

</AccordionItem>
<AccordionItem question="Where the load figures come from">

Your server reports its load average with every sync answer: how many processes were running or waiting for a processor, averaged over the last minute. Compare it with your server's number of processor cores. A load of 4 on a 4-core server means it's fully busy; on a 16-core server it's light.

The **elevated** and **high** words work this out for you. WCPOS divides the load by the core count: under 0.9 per core is low, up to 1.8 is elevated, above that is high. On shared hosting the figure counts every site on the machine, not just yours.

**Your server doesn't report its load. Everything else on this page still works.** appears in place of the chart when PHP can't read the load average. That's normal on Windows servers and on hosts that switch the function off. The pace line still works without it, because the till also watches errors and answer times.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## When your server keeps struggling {#fix}

Work down this list. Each step is cheaper than the next. Anyone at the till can do step 1. Steps 2 to 4 are for whoever looks after your website: the store owner, a developer or your host.

1. **Choose a lighter preset.** On tills that don't need to see each other's sales within seconds, open **Store health › Performance** and choose **Eco**. Avoid **Realtime** on shared hosting. The preset is saved per till. See [Sync presets](../store-health.mdx#sync-presets).
2. **Switch WooCommerce to HPOS** if it still uses legacy order storage. It's the biggest single gain on a store with many orders. See [Order Storage (HPOS)](./order-storage.mdx#switch-to-hpos).
3. **Look for the plugin that's slowing every request.** Test on a staging site with only WooCommerce and WCPOS active, then switch plugins back on one at a time. See [Plugin Conflicts](../troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx#step-2-the-plugin-elimination-method).
4. **Ask your host** for object caching (Redis or Memcached), more PHP workers and memory, or a move off shared hosting. See [What to send your host](#when-to-seek-help).

## What to send your host {#when-to-seek-help}

Contact your host or a WordPress developer when **Easing off** or **Paused until …** shows most days, the typical response stays over 2 seconds, or your host's logs show memory errors. Send them:

- A screenshot of **Store health → Performance**, scrolled to **Your server, over time**.
- The debug report from **Store health → Logs → Copy debug info** (**Share debug info** in the phone and tablet apps). See [Logs](../store-health.mdx#logs).
- Your server's specification: cores, memory and storage type.
- Your list of active plugins, and your WordPress and WooCommerce versions.


## Server requirements {#minimum-server-requirements}

WCPOS installs on any site that meets the [plugin's minimums](../../getting-started/installation.mdx#server-requirements): PHP 7.4, WordPress 5.6 and WooCommerce 9.0. A store selling every day needs more; the figures to send your host are below.

<AccordionItem question="Server requirements to send your host">

For a store selling every day, we recommend at least:

  <RequirementItem label="CPU">4 or more cores</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="RAM">4 GB or more</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="PHP">PHP 8.x, with OPcache on</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="Database">MySQL 8.0 or later, or MariaDB 11</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="Storage">SSD or NVMe</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="SSL">HTTPS with a valid certificate</RequirementItem>
</RequirementsList>

</AccordionItem>

:::warning Shared hosting is often not enough
Shared hosting usually has too few PHP workers and too little memory for several tills checking at once. For a store selling every day, use a VPS or a managed WordPress host. WordPress.com hosting has had REST API problems, such as only 9 or 10 products loading from a large catalogue; self-hosted WordPress avoids them.
:::

<AccordionItem question="The stack stores report as fastest">

Store owners report the best POS speed with this combination:

- **MariaDB 11.4**
- **HPOS** for orders ([Order Storage](./order-storage.mdx))
- **Redis** or LiteSpeed object caching
- **NVMe** storage
- **PHP OPcache** on

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Errors your server sends {#503-errors}

### 503 Service Unavailable {#503}

Your server, or something in front of it, couldn't handle the request. Check the server's load and free memory, whether its PHP workers are used up, and that PHP's memory limit is **256 MB** or more. Your host's error logs show what the POS can't. WCPOS answers a short 503 of its own while the plugin is being updated or installing its tables; the till waits and tries again by itself.

### 429 Too Many Requests {#429}

Your host or firewall limits how often one address can call your site. The till eases off at once, but several tills behind one shop router share an address. Ask your host to allow more requests to `/wp-json/`, and choose **Eco** on tills that don't need speed.

### 414 URI Too Long {#http-414-uri-too-long}

You see it on a payment page under the checkout's **Legacy** tab. That page opens with your sign-in in its address, and your server's limit on address length is too short. Ask whoever looks after your website, or your host, to raise it.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="For your website maintainer: raising the address limit">

- **Apache:** `LimitRequestLine 65536` in `httpd.conf`
- **Nginx:** `large_client_header_buffers 4 65536;` in `nginx.conf`

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Update WooCommerce safely {#woocommerce-version-compatibility}

A WooCommerce update can occasionally break the POS. Update WooCommerce on a [staging site](../troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx#before-you-start-use-a-staging-site) first, and keep WCPOS up to date so it has the latest compatibility fixes. WCPOS 2.0 needs WooCommerce 9.0 or later.

If an update breaks the POS, tell us on [Discord](https://wcpos.com/discord), and ask whoever looks after your website about going back. An update can change your store's database, so they should check that first and have a backup from before the update.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="For your website maintainer: going back to an earlier WooCommerce">

The **Advanced View** link on WooCommerce's WordPress.org page lists previous versions. Restore from the backup taken before the update when the update changed the database; otherwise install the version you had before. Try it on staging first.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Order storage (HPOS) {#woocommerce-high-performance-order-storage-hpos}

WooCommerce's **High-performance order storage** keeps orders in their own tables instead of among your posts. On a store with many orders, saving a sale is much faster with it. Check which storage your store uses, and switch with WooCommerce's own tool: [Order Storage (HPOS)](./order-storage.mdx). WooCommerce's guide is [High-Performance Order Storage](https://woocommerce.com/document/high-performance-order-storage/).

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="PHP and WordPress settings to ask your host about">

These are starting points, not rules. Your host knows what its servers allow.

```ini
memory_limit = 512M
max_execution_time = 300
max_input_vars = 3000
```

In `wp-config.php`, keeping fewer post revisions keeps the database smaller:

```php
define('WP_POST_REVISIONS', 3);
```

Plugins that often slow every request: heavy SEO plugins running on product saves, complex inventory systems, real-time analytics and tracking, and custom code hooked into WooCommerce. Find yours with [Plugin Conflicts](../troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx).

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Hosting-specific notes {#hosting-specific-notes}

Some hosts and CDNs need a change before the POS can reach your site's REST API. Open yours:

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="GoDaddy">

The website firewall blocks `/wp-json/`, with errors like _"Received 'undefined'"_. In **Website Security › Firewall › Settings › Access Control › Allow URL Paths**, add `/wp-json/`.

</AccordionItem>
<AccordionItem question="WP Engine">

The REST API has to be switched on. Switch it on, and allow its paths in the WP Engine firewall if needed.

</AccordionItem>
<AccordionItem question="Cloudflare">

Bot Fight Mode, Under Attack Mode or WAF challenges block REST requests, and the cache can serve stale answers. Follow the [Cloudflare guide](../troubleshooting/cloudflare.mdx) to add a WAF **Skip** rule and a **Bypass cache** rule for `/wp-json/`.

</AccordionItem>
<AccordionItem question="WordPress.com">

Products can fail to load, such as only 9 or 10 showing from a large catalogue. Use self-hosted WordPress.

</AccordionItem>
<AccordionItem question="Shared hosting">

Too few PHP workers and too little memory for several tills. Move to a VPS or a managed WordPress host.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

Security plugins such as Wordfence can block the REST API the same way. See [Plugin Conflicts](../troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx).

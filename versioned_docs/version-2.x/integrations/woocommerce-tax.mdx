# WooCommerce Tax

[WooCommerce Tax](https://woocommerce.com/document/woocommerce-tax/) looks up sales tax for you through TaxJar, so you don't keep a rate table by hand. It works with WCPOS: POS sales get the same rates as your online orders, and there is nothing to switch on in WCPOS.

## How POS orders get their rates {#how-it-works}

WooCommerce Tax only looks up rates at your online checkout and on the WooCommerce order screen. POS orders reach your store another way, so WCPOS asks WooCommerce Tax for them:

1. Each time the POS sends an open order to your store, such as when you press **Checkout**, WCPOS asks WooCommerce Tax for the rates at the order's tax address. That address follows **Calculate tax based on**: see [Which address is taxed](#which-address).
2. WooCommerce Tax adds those rates to your store's tax rate table, one row per jurisdiction, for that postcode and city. You'll find them under `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Settings > Tax`.
3. WooCommerce taxes the order with those rows. It carries one tax line per jurisdiction, such as a state tax and a county tax.
4. The POS downloads the new rows on its next check for changes, so the next sale at that address adds up the same on the till and on your store.

Adding or removing items on an open order works its tax out again from the items now on it. Once an order is paid, WCPOS leaves it alone and WooCommerce Tax treats it like any other order.

## Set it up {#setup}

1. **Connect WooCommerce Tax.** Install and activate it, then connect your site to WordPress.com. `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Status > WooCommerce Tax` should say **Connected to WordPress.com**.
2. **Turn on automated taxes.** Under `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Settings > Tax`, set **Automated taxes** to **Enable automated taxes**. Your store's country must be one WooCommerce Tax supports.
3. **Check the POS.** The POS copies your WooCommerce tax settings each time it loads your store, so there's nothing to change. Open **Settings**, then **Tax**, and check the rows below.
4. **Make a test sale.** Ring up a taxable product and press **Checkout**. In `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Orders`, the order should carry one tax line per jurisdiction, and the matching rows should now be in your tax rate table. Then void the sale or finish it.

:::warning Rates you entered by hand
Turning on automated taxes backs up the rates you entered by hand to a CSV file, then removes them from your rate table. Download the backup from `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Status > WooCommerce Tax` if you might need them again.
:::

## Which address is taxed {#which-address}

| Your POS | Without a customer | With a customer |
|---|---|---|
| **WCPOS** (free) | your shop address | their shipping address, else their billing address, else your shop address |
| **WCPOS Pro** | the store's **Calculate tax using** address | the same, unless that store is set to the customer's billing or shipping address |

WCPOS Pro sets the address per store in `WP Admin > POS > Stores`, on the store's **Tax** page. A new store uses **This store's address**. See [Setting Up Stores](../stores/setup.mdx#store-pages).

## What to expect at the till {#what-to-expect}

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={2} alt="The POS with one product in the cart and the tax address panel open: the address the cart is taxed at and the rates that match it" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Tax address** — the button under the products, such as **Shop base** or **Customer shipping address**. Press it to see the address the cart is taxed at and the rates that match it.
2. **Change in Settings › Tax** — opens the POS Tax settings.
3. **View all tax rates** — every rate this device has downloaded. See [Check the tax rates](../settings/store/tax.mdx#view-all-tax-rates).
4. **Tax** — the cart's tax, one line per jurisdiction when **Display Tax Totals** is **Itemized**.
5. **Checkout** — sends the order to your store, which asks WooCommerce Tax for its rates.

</div>

**The first sale at a new address can disagree once.** The POS adds up a sale from the rate rows it has already downloaded. If your store has never taxed that address, there is no row for it yet, so the POS can show the [totals notice](../support/troubleshooting/totals-disagree.mdx) on that one sale. Your store's figures are what is recorded. Once the row reaches the POS, later sales there agree. With tax based on a customer address, this can happen once for each new postcode. With your shop or store address, normally only on the very first sale. A jurisdiction changing its rate can cause it once more.

**Don't add rate rows by hand.** If the tax address button turns red and its panel says **No rate matches it**, or **View all tax rates** has nothing for an address, nothing has been sold there yet. The first sale adds the rows.

### Get new rates onto the till straight away {#fetch-now}

The POS checks your store for changes every minute or so, depending on its [sync preset](../support/store-health.mdx#sync-presets). To fetch new rows now, open **Store health**, then **Database**, and press **⋮** on the **Tax rates** row.

1. **Tax rates** — how many rates are on this device and on your store.
2. **Check for changes now** — fetches new and changed rates. Use this first.
3. **Clear & re-download** — drops this device's copy of the rates and downloads them all again. See [Clear & re-download](../support/store-health.mdx#clear-and-redownload).

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

**The totals notice shows on every sale at the same address.** The rows are not reaching the POS. Use **Check for changes now** on the **Tax rates** row, then make another sale there. If it still disagrees, see [Your Store and the POS Disagree on Totals](../support/troubleshooting/totals-disagree.mdx).

**An order has no tax from WooCommerce Tax.** A failed lookup never blocks a sale: if TaxJar can't be reached, or WooCommerce Tax returns no rates for the order, the order saves with the rates already in your table. WCPOS writes which one it was to its log on your server: *WooCommerce Tax rate priming failed* when the lookup failed, *WooCommerce Tax returned no rates for the POS order* when it returned nothing. See [Logs](../support/logs.mdx#wp-admin-logs-page).

**A fee is taxed at an unexpected rate.** WCPOS doesn't send fees to TaxJar. A taxable fee is taxed with the rate your table already holds for the fee's tax class.

**You use Stripe Tax.** Stripe Tax does not work with POS orders. See [Plugin Conflicts](../support/troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx).

# WooCommerce Tax

[WooCommerce Tax](https://woocommerce.com/document/woocommerce-tax/) looks up sales tax for you through TaxJar, so you don't keep a rate table by hand. It works with WCPOS: POS sales get the same rates as your online orders, and there is nothing to switch on in WCPOS.

Already set up? See [What to expect at the till](#what-to-expect).

## Requirements {#requirements}

<RequirementsList>
  <RequirementItem label="WooCommerce Tax">Installed, connected to WordPress.com, with automated taxes on</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="Store country">One WooCommerce Tax supports for automated taxes</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="WooCommerce">7.6 or newer. On older WooCommerce, WCPOS doesn't ask WooCommerce Tax for rates, and POS orders use the rows already in your table</RequirementItem>
  <RequirementItem label="WCPOS">Free and Pro</RequirementItem>
</RequirementsList>

## Set it up {#setup}

:::warning Rates you entered by hand
Turning on automated taxes (step 2) backs up the rates you entered by hand to a CSV file, then removes them from your rate table. Download the backup from `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Status > WooCommerce Tax` if you might need them again.
:::

1. **Connect WooCommerce Tax.** Install and activate it, then connect your site to WordPress.com. `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Status > WooCommerce Tax` should say **Connected to WordPress.com**.
2. **Turn on automated taxes.** Under `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Settings > Tax`, set **Automated taxes** to **Enable automated taxes**.
3. **Check the POS.** The POS copies your WooCommerce tax settings each time it loads your store, so there's nothing to change. Open **Settings**, then **Tax**, and check the rows below.
4. **Make a test sale.** Ring up a taxable product and press **Checkout**. In `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Orders`, the order should carry one tax line per jurisdiction, and the matching rows should now be in your tax rate table. Then void the sale or finish it.

   If the POS shows **Your store changed this order's totals** after **Checkout**, take payment for your store's **Total**, the second amount on the **Total** line. The first sale at a new address can do this once: see [What to expect at the till](#what-to-expect).

While automated taxes are on, WooCommerce Tax sets your store to work out tax from the customer's shipping address, with prices entered and shown without tax, and locks those settings. The POS Tax page should look like this:

**Calculate tax based on**, at the top, is which address sets the rate. See [Which address is taxed](#which-address). If a row on your till doesn't match WooCommerce, see [Tax Settings](../settings/store/tax.mdx). **Restore server settings**, under **Settings** then **General**, copies your store's values onto this device again.

## Which address is taxed {#which-address}

**WCPOS (free)**

- Without a customer: your shop address.
- With a customer: their shipping address, else their billing address, else your shop address.

**WCPOS Pro** sets the address per store, as **Calculate tax using** on the store's **Tax** page in `WP Admin > POS > Stores`. A new store uses **This store's address**. See [Setting Up Stores](../stores/setup.mdx#store-pages).

- Store set to its own address or your shop address: every sale is taxed there, with a customer or without.
- Store set to the customer's billing or shipping address: a sale with a customer is taxed at that address, as in the free version. Without a customer, your shop address.

## What to expect at the till {#what-to-expect}

A sale looks the same as any other. The cart works out tax from the rate rows the POS has downloaded, and your store asks WooCommerce Tax for the rates when you press **Checkout**.

:::warning The first sale at a new address can disagree once
The POS has no rate row for an address your store has never taxed, so the cart's tax can differ from your store's. After **Checkout**, the POS shows **Your store changed this order's totals**, with each amount as *POS → store*. Take payment for your store's **Total**, the second amount on the **Total** line, and check it before handing over the goods.
:::

1. **Subtotal and tax** — prices are without tax, so the tax is added on top. With **Display Tax Totals** set to **Itemized**, each jurisdiction gets its own line.
2. **Checkout** — sends the order to your store, which asks WooCommerce Tax for its rates.

To see where the cart is taxed, press the tax address button under the products, such as **Shop base** or **Customer shipping address**:

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={3} offsets={[[390, 0], [160, 0], [143, 0]]} alt="The tax address panel open over the products: the address the cart is calculated from and the rates that match it" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Address and rates** — where the tax is calculated from, and every rate on this device that matches it.
2. **Change in Settings › Tax** — opens the POS Tax settings.
3. **View all tax rates** — every rate this device has downloaded. See [Check the tax rates](../settings/store/tax.mdx#view-all-tax-rates).

**How often the totals notice shows.** Once the new row reaches the POS, later sales at that address agree. With tax based on a customer address, expect the notice once for each new postcode. With your shop or store address, normally only on the very first sale. A jurisdiction changing its rate can cause it once more. See [Totals disagree](../support/troubleshooting/totals-disagree.mdx).

**Don't add rate rows by hand.** If the tax address button turns red and its panel says **No rate matches it**, or **View all tax rates** has nothing for an address, nothing has been sold there yet. The first sale adds the rows.

### Get new rates onto the till straight away {#fetch-now}

The POS checks your store for changes every minute or so, depending on its [sync preset](../support/store-health.mdx#sync-presets). To fetch new rows now, open **Store health**, then **Database**, and press **⋮** on the **Tax rates** row.

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Tax rates** — how many rates are on this device and on your store.
2. **Check for changes now** — fetches new and changed rates. Use this first.
3. **Clear & re-download** — drops this device's copy of the rates and downloads them all again. See [Clear & re-download](../support/store-health.mdx#clear-and-redownload).

</div>

## How POS orders get their rates {#how-it-works}

WooCommerce Tax only looks up rates at your online checkout and on the WooCommerce order screen. POS orders reach your store another way, so WCPOS asks WooCommerce Tax for them.

<details>
<summary>What happens when you press Checkout</summary>

1. Each time the POS sends an open order to your store, such as when you press **Checkout**, WCPOS asks WooCommerce Tax for the rates at the order's tax address. That address follows **Calculate tax based on**: see [Which address is taxed](#which-address).
2. WooCommerce Tax adds those rates to your store's tax rate table, one row per jurisdiction, for that postcode and city. You'll find them under `WP Admin > WooCommerce > Settings > Tax`.
3. WooCommerce taxes the order with those rows. It carries one tax line per jurisdiction, such as a state tax and a county tax.
4. The POS downloads the new rows on its next check for changes, so the next sale at that address adds up the same on the till and on your store.

Adding or removing items on an open order works its tax out again from the items now on it. Once an order is paid, WCPOS leaves it alone and WooCommerce Tax treats it like any other order.

</details>

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

**The totals notice shows on every sale at the same address.** The rows are not reaching the POS. Use **Check for changes now** on the **Tax rates** row, then make another sale there. If it still disagrees, see [Your Store and the POS Disagree on Totals](../support/troubleshooting/totals-disagree.mdx).

**An order has no tax from WooCommerce Tax.** The sale still saves, with the rates already in your table. Check the WCPOS log on your server to see why: see [Logs](../support/logs.mdx#wp-admin-logs-page).

<details>
<summary>The two log messages</summary>

A failed lookup never blocks a sale. WCPOS writes one of these to its log:

- *WooCommerce Tax rate priming failed*: the lookup failed, for example because TaxJar couldn't be reached.
- *WooCommerce Tax returned no rates for the POS order*: WooCommerce Tax answered with no rates for the order.

</details>

**A fee is taxed at an unexpected rate.** WCPOS doesn't send fees to TaxJar. A taxable fee is taxed with the rate your table already holds for the fee's tax class.

**You use Stripe Tax.** Stripe Tax does not work with POS orders. See [Plugin Conflicts](../support/troubleshooting/plugin-conflicts.mdx).

Every till keeps checking your store for changes. Most stores run on shared hosting, where each of those requests starts WordPress from scratch and competes with your website's own visitors. So the POS treats your server's time as something to spend carefully: an unchanged store costs almost nothing to keep in sync, background work has fixed limits, and the till slows itself down when your server struggles.

This page gives the numbers behind those promises, for store owners and developers. To change the settings on a till, see [Store health › Performance](../support/store-health.mdx#performance).

## The Performance tab, and what sits behind it {#performance-tab}

Open **Store health** from the pulse icon at the foot of the menu, then **Performance**. The settings are saved on this device only, for the store it is on.

## Four rules the engine follows {#the-four-rules}

1. **Cost follows what changed, not how big your catalogue is.** Every check starts with a cheap question, and an unchanged store answers it with an empty reply. An unchanged 50,000-product store costs about the same to keep in sync as an unchanged 500-product store.
2. **Background work has a ceiling.** Every background maintenance job declares the most requests it may make in one run, and the engine's tests fail if it makes more. Bigger jobs run in small batches and pick up where they left off next time.
3. **The cashier comes first.** The requests that make a till ready to sell run first. Audits wait until the first page of products has downloaded, then a further minute. While the app is hidden or in the background, maintenance waits.
4. **Under pressure, background work gives way first.** When your server struggles, the till checks for changes less often and puts off maintenance. Searches, barcode lookups and saving a sale are never held back.

## What syncing costs your server {#request-budgets}

Two settings set the steady cost: how often the till checks, and how many records it fetches per request.

| Preset | Check for changes | Records per request | Checks per hour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Eco** | every 5 min | 25 | 12 |
| **Balanced** (default) | every 60 s | 50 | 60 |
| **Realtime** | every 10 s | 75 | 360 |
| Custom | 5 s to 5 min, in 5 s steps | 10 to 100, in steps of 5 | 720 to 12 |

Moving either slider makes the setting **Custom**; **Reset to Balanced** puts the default back.

### Page weight {#page-weight}

Records per request decides how much work each download is for your server. A page of 100 products can take seconds even on a fast server, and the check interval only multiplies that. So each preset moves both settings together, and no preset uses the 100-record maximum: it is there in **Custom** for servers you know can take it.

### What keeps a quiet store cheap {#cheap-checks}

- **An unchanged store answers with an empty reply.** The till sends what it last saw, and the server replies `304 Not Modified` with no body. It still counts as one request.
- **Checks are spread out.** Each wait is between 80% and 120% of your setting, so several tills don't all ask at the same moment. That's why the **Right now** countdown is rarely exactly your setting.
- **A till nobody is using slows down,** on settings faster than a minute. After 10 minutes with no tap, key press or barcode scan, it steps to a check every 30 seconds, then every 60 seconds. A hidden app goes straight to 60 seconds. Any use brings it back and checks at once. **Balanced** and **Eco** are already at a minute or slower, so they don't change.

### Background maintenance {#maintenance-budgets}

On top of the change checks, a few background jobs keep the till's copy complete and honest: they find records deleted on the server, and fill in what a till hasn't downloaded yet, so it can sell offline. Each run has a fixed ceiling.

| Job | Runs about every | Most requests per run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deletion audit | 17 min | 11 |
| Deletion audit, till already in step | 17 min | summary pages only (at most 9), no detail requests |
| Fingerprints for records already on the till (100 IDs a request) | 15 min | 5 |
| Product top-up, one page in the order the product list is sorted | 5 min | 1 |
| Customer top-up | 5 min | 1 |
| Variation download | 5 min | 1 |

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="How the deletion audit stays inside 11 requests">

The audit compares the till's records with the server in buckets. It reads at most 9 summary pages (3 for each kind of record it checks), and skips every bucket whose summary matches. Only a bucket that differs gets a detail request, and at most 2 of those per run. If there is more to check, the audit saves its place and carries on next run, rather than catching up in one burst. Under server pressure a run drops to 1 summary page per kind and 1 detail request.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

### The requests-per-hour figure {#nominal-rate}

**At these settings: ~N requests per hour** is worked out from **Check for changes** alone. Treat it as the nominal check rate, not a limit. Spreading moves the real count either way, a `304` still counts, an idle till on a fast setting asks less, and your server slowing the till down asks less again. Background maintenance and the cashier's own requests (searches, saving orders) come on top. The **last 24 h** count at the top of the tab is the real total.

## Backing off when your server struggles {#server-pressure}

Every answer the till gets feeds a pressure check, one per website on this device. When it trips, the pace line under **Your server, over time** says so, with the reason.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={2} offsets={[[91, 0], [91, -84], [106, -84], [165, 0]]} alt="Your server, over time: the pace line reading Normal pace, the Server load and POS requests charts, both still filling in, and the Learn more in the docs link" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Pace** — **Normal pace** here. The other lines are in the table below.
2. **Server load** — the load your server reports, everything running on it and not just the POS. A server that doesn't send it shows **Your server doesn't report its load**, and the rest of the tab still works.
3. **POS requests** — this till's requests, hour by hour, for the last 24 hours. Both charts read **Not enough data yet** until the till has run for a while, as here.
4. **Learn more in the docs** — opens the docs on how products sync.

</div>

| What your server does | When the till reacts | Pace line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answers `429 Too Many Requests` | At once | **Easing off — checking 2× less often: your server asked the POS to slow down** |
| Returns `5xx` errors | 3 within a minute | **… your server returned errors** |
| Doesn't answer in time | 3 within a minute | **… requests to your server timed out** |
| Answers slowly | Middle of the last 10 response times above 2 seconds | **… your server was answering slowly** |
| Marks its answers `X-WCPOS-Pressure: high` | On about 6 of the last 10 | **… your server reported high load** |
| Sends `Retry-After` with a refusal | At once, for up to 15 minutes | **Paused until … — your server asked the POS to wait** |

Errors while the device is offline don't count: that's your connection, not your server.

A server that marks its answers `elevated` or `high` but still answers quickly shows **Normal pace — your server reports high load; the till only slows down if its answers do**. The header can start a slowdown but never keep one going on its own: the till trusts how fast your server actually answers.

### What slowing down means {#back-off-steps}

- **Each step doubles the wait between checks,** up to about 5 minutes: **Realtime** can slow 32×, **Balanced** 8×, and **Eco** 2× (to 10 minutes). A custom setting slows to 5 minutes, or to twice its own interval if that is longer. A `Retry-After` pause can run past those limits, up to its 15 minutes.
- **Background maintenance waits.** The jobs in the table above hold off. A job that has waited twice its usual interval (the deletion audit, once) does one run anyway, within its ceiling, unless your server has asked the POS to wait. The deletion audit's run is cut to 1 summary page per kind of record and 1 detail request.
- **The cashier is never slowed.** Searches, barcode lookups, opening a record and saving a sale go straight through.

### Coming back up {#recovery}

The till speeds up one step after **10 healthy answers in a row**, and only once a minute has passed since it last slowed down and no `Retry-After` pause is running. Slowing down is instant; speeding up is gradual, so a shaky server isn't bounced between fast and slow. From 32× it takes 50 healthy answers to get back to your setting.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="Presets, store switches, restarts and server load">

- **Changing preset keeps the slowdown,** cut down to the new preset's limit. You can't switch it off: it runs under every preset, including **Custom**.
- **Switching store or cashier on the same website keeps it.** Each website on the device has its own.
- **Closing the app resets it.** The till starts at your setting and slows again if your server is still struggling.
- **Server load works on its own.** When the load your server reports reaches twice its usual level (and at least 1) on two readings, the till checks half as often, until two readings fall back under 1.25 times its usual level.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Staying responsive on the till {#device-responsiveness}

Background sync must never make the screen stutter.

- **Audits pause to let the screen respond.** The deletion audit works in chunks, and no chunk runs longer than about **30 ms**, well under the 100 ms where people notice lag. With 10,000 products on the till, a whole audit takes about **68 ms** of work, spread over 17 or 18 pauses. With 50,000 products, under 300 ms.
- **The first result stays quick while sync runs.** The first results of a query still arrive in about **7–10 ms** while a 10,000-product audit runs, and about **40–54 ms** during a 50,000-product audit.
- **Only the rows you can see leave the database,** when the filter and sort can run inside it, so a screen update moves ten rows, not ten thousand. Searches, and filters the database can't run itself, are worked out on the till instead.

These figures come from the engine's performance tests, measured on an Apple silicon computer with the database in memory. The tests run on their own in CI whenever the sync engine changes. Their limits sit about 11–18 times above the measured figures (3 times for the 30 ms chunk), and three times higher again in CI, so they catch a slowdown of that size, not a slow test machine.

## Checked against a live store {#live-verification}

The rules above are also checked on a real WordPress store. After a release is deployed, a test run by hand reloads the POS with an audit due and checks that:

- the till makes **no** audit requests before the product list first shows, and
- after the minute's hold, the audit makes at most **9** summary pages and **2** detail requests.

The test came from a real regression: an early development build sent **41** detail requests (**1.2 MB**) in one audit when a till rebuilt its records. The design that replaced it checks summaries first and keeps detail requests under the ceilings above, and the engine's tests enforce those ceilings on every change.

## What the numbers don't tell you {#honest-limits}

- **How long a first download takes.** That depends almost entirely on your server's response times and your catalogue size. In the tests, writing 10,000 downloaded products into the till's database takes under 10 ms; the network and the server take the rest.
- **How much faster than WCPOS 1.x this is.** The two designs differ too much for one number to be fair. [How the Sync Engine Works](./sync-engine.mdx) lays out how they differ.
- **What your own store will see.** The test figures come from one computer with the database in memory. That's why the Performance tab shows what this till actually did in the last 24 hours rather than a forecast. Its data figure counts only answers that state their size, so it can read lower than the real traffic, even **0.0 MB**.

## If your server still struggles {#troubleshooting}

- **Easing off** or **Paused until …** shows most days — your hosting is short of capacity for what it is serving. Choose **Eco** on each till, and see [Server Performance](../support/performance/server.mdx) for hosting, caching and slow-response checks.
- **⚠ Needs attention** on the status line — the till is offline, sync is blocked, or records have errors. See [Store health](../support/store-health.mdx#performance).
- **Your server doesn't report its load** — nothing is wrong. The pace and the request counts still work.
- **0.0 MB** next to hundreds of requests — your server doesn't state the size of its answers, so they count as 0. See [What the numbers don't tell you](#honest-limits).

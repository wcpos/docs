When a cashier takes money off a sale, WCPOS records it on the WooCommerce order in one of three places. This page shows where, so a report, an export or another plugin reads it right.

## Take money off at the till {#at-the-till}

The cashier has three ways to take money off. Use one, or combine them. Screenshots and every option are on [Cart Discounts](../pos/cart/discounts.mdx).

### Change one item's price {#change-price}

1. Tap the price on a line. A keypad opens.
2. Press a quick discount key, or type the new price.
3. Press **Done**. The line total changes to the quantity × the new price.

More: [Take money off one item](../pos/cart/discounts.mdx#quick-discounts).

### Add Discount {#add-discount}

1. Press **+** at the top of the cart and choose **Add Discount**.
2. On **Quick discount**, type the amount and choose **$** or **%**.
3. Press **Add Discount**. A **Discount (10%)** row, or **Discount** for a fixed amount, appears with the totals.

It can't be combined with a coupon marked **Individual use only**; see [Add Discount lines](#add-discount-lines). More: [Take money off the whole order](../pos/cart/discounts.mdx#order-level-discounts).

### Apply a coupon {#apply-coupon}

1. Press **+** at the top of the cart and choose **Add Discount**.
2. Switch to **Coupon** and find the coupon in **Select coupon**.
3. Press **Apply**. The POS checks the coupon's rules and puts it on the order, or says why it can't be used.

More: [Apply a coupon](../pos/cart/discounts.mdx#coupon).

## Where each discount is recorded {#where-recorded}

Each way lands in a different place on the order:

- **A changed price** is the line's price: the `_woocommerce_pos_data` meta and the line `subtotal`. It is **not** in `discount_total`, and no coupon rule applies to it.
- **Add Discount** is a coupon line with the code `pos-discount` and `_wcpos_quick_discount` meta. It **is** in `discount_total`. It applies to every line, sale items included, but can't be combined with a coupon marked **Individual use only**.
- **A store coupon** is a coupon line with the coupon's code. It **is** in `discount_total`, and the coupon's own rules apply.

## How POS price overrides are stored {#how-pos-price-overrides-are-stored}

Every product line the POS creates carries the per-unit prices in the `_woocommerce_pos_data` line item meta:

```json
{
  "price": "40.00",
  "regular_price": "45.00",
  "tax_status": "taxable"
}
```

- `price` is what the line is sold at, after any change at the till.
- `regular_price` is the product's regular price when it was added. Changing the price at the till never changes it, so a price raised at the till sits above it.
- A [misc product](../pos/cart/index.mdx#miscellaneous-products) also carries `virtual`, `downloadable` and `categories`, so coupon rules can check them.

This meta is the authoritative per-unit price. The line's `subtotal` is worked out from `price` × quantity, with tax taken out and rounding applied, and `total` drops below it when a coupon or **Add Discount** applies. For per-unit precision, read `price` and `regular_price` from the meta.

The meta is hidden in WooCommerce's order screen.

<details>
<summary>Reading the meta from code</summary>

**Use a decoder that takes both shapes.** Lines from older apps hold the meta as a JSON string. The 2.0 app writes it as an object, so WordPress stores a serialized array, and values may be numbers rather than strings. A bare `json_decode()` fails on the array.

New POS orders also carry the private `_woocommerce_pos_version` order meta: the version of the WCPOS plugin that saved the order. Receipts still treat the stored line shape as authoritative, because an order taken offline may reach a newer plugin than the app that made it.

</details>

## Line totals and the Discount total {#subtotal-model-v190}

WCPOS records a till price the way WooCommerce records a sale price:

- `line_item.subtotal` = `price` × quantity, where `price` is the till price.
- `line_item.total` = `subtotal` − the line's share of coupons and **Add Discount**.
- `order.discount_total` = coupons and **Add Discount** lines only. A till price is never in it.

A worked example, with no tax: a watch with a regular price of $45.00, its price typed as $40.00 at the till, then a 10% **Add Discount**. $45.00 → $40.00 at the till → $40.00 − $4.00 = **$36.00**. The order stores it as:

- `_woocommerce_pos_data.regular_price`: **45.00**
- `_woocommerce_pos_data.price`: **40.00**, the till price
- `line_items[0].subtotal`: **40.00**, 40.00 × 1
- `line_items[0].total`: **36.00**, less the **Add Discount**
- `discount_total`: **4.00**, the **Add Discount** only
- `total`: **36.00**

The $5.00 the till price took off shows only by comparing `regular_price` with `price`. Receipts do that comparison for you; see [Receipt data](#receipt-data-exposure).

<details>
<summary>Orders from before v1.9.0</summary>

Before v1.9.0 the app sent `subtotal` = `regular_price` × quantity. WooCommerce then worked out `discount_total` = `subtotal` − `total`, so a till price counted as a discount. That broke coupon maths: `recalculate_coupons()` works from `subtotal`, so coupons were calculated on the original price. A server-side `woocommerce_order_item_get_subtotal` filter worked around it during `recalculate_coupons()`.

v1.9.0 moved to the sale-price model above and removed the filter. Orders saved before then keep their old figures, which is why a report can show a till price inside `discount_total` on an old order. Receipts allow for it: see [Receipt data](#receipt-data-exposure).

</details>

## Add Discount lines {#add-discount-lines}

**Add Discount** takes an amount or a percentage off the whole order. It is a coupon line, not a coupon in your store:

- **Code** — `pos-discount` for the first on an order, then `pos-discount-2`, `pos-discount-3` and so on.
- **Meta** — `_wcpos_quick_discount` on the coupon line: `{ "discount_type": "percent", "amount": "10" }`. `discount_type` is `percent` (up to 100) or `fixed_cart`; `amount` is a positive decimal string.
- **Rules** — it applies to sale items, including prices lowered at the till. It can't be combined with a coupon marked **Individual use only**.
- **Recalculate** — WCPOS stores the coupon details on the line, so **Recalculate** in WooCommerce's order screen keeps the discount.

The virtual coupon exists only while WCPOS saves a POS order: the storefront can't redeem `pos-discount`. If a store coupon already has a code the request uses, the save is refused with **Quick discount code "…" belongs to a store coupon. Use another code.**

The 2.0 app no longer takes money off with a negative fee: it asks for **Add Discount** instead. The plugin still accepts a negative fee line from older apps and other clients.

## Coupon Interaction Behaviour {#coupon-interaction-behaviour}

Applying a store coupon at the till works on the free plugin; creating one at the till needs [WCPOS Pro](../getting-started/pro-license.mdx). When a coupon is applied to an order that has a till price:

1. The coupon works from the **till price** (`_woocommerce_pos_data.price`), not `regular_price`.
2. Removing the coupon returns the line to its till price.

The app works coupons out on the device, so it can apply one offline, and the plugin works them out again with the same product context when it saves the order.

### `exclude_sale_items` Behaviour {#exclude_sale_items-behaviour}

A line whose `_woocommerce_pos_data.price` is below its `regular_price` counts as on sale, the same as a WooCommerce sale price. A price raised at the till doesn't. So a coupon with **Exclude sale items**:

- skips a line whose price was lowered at the till;
- can't be used at all while one is in the cart, if it is a fixed cart coupon. This is WooCommerce's own rule for fixed cart coupons.

To change what counts as on sale, use the `wcpos_item_is_on_sale` filter below.

## Available Filters {#available-filters}

### `wcpos_item_is_on_sale` {#wcpos_item_is_on_sale}

Override whether a line with POS price data counts as "on sale" when the plugin validates coupons. Before WCPOS 2.0 this filter was named `woocommerce_pos_item_is_on_sale`; that name still works but is deprecated since 2.0 and is removed in 2.2 at the earliest (see [Hook naming](../extensions/index.mdx#hook-naming)).

<details>
<summary>Example and parameters</summary>

```php
add_filter( 'wcpos_item_is_on_sale', function ( $is_on_sale, $product, $item, $pos_data ) {
    // Let coupons with exclude_sale_items apply to lines lowered at the till.
    return false;
}, 10, 4 );
```

- `$is_on_sale` (`bool`) — whether the line counts as on sale. Default: `price < regular_price`.
- `$product` (`WC_Product`) — the product object.
- `$item` (`WC_Order_Item_Product`) — the order line item.
- `$pos_data` (`array`) — the decoded `_woocommerce_pos_data`.

</details>

The filter runs on the server. The app's own on-sale check, used while the cashier is still building the cart, is always `price < regular_price`; the server's answer applies when the order is saved.

## REST API Endpoint {#rest-api-endpoint}

Coupon endpoints live in the **free plugin**, so the app never gets a 404 when it asks for coupons, even without WCPOS Pro.

The 2.0 app syncs coupons through `GET /wp-json/wcpos/v2/coupons`, which forwards to WooCommerce's `wc/v3/coupons` with the POS read permission. A coupon made at the till (Pro) is saved as an ordinary WooCommerce coupon, and the cashier needs permission to create coupons.

<details>
<summary>The v1 endpoint, for apps before 2.0</summary>

Apps before 2.0 use `/wp-json/wcpos/v1/coupons`. Its controller extends `WC_REST_Coupons_Controller` and adds UUIDs for offline sync, read access for any user with the `access_woocommerce_pos` capability, and a fast ID-only path when `posts_per_page=-1` and `fields=id` (or `fields=id,date_modified_gmt`) are requested.

</details>

For who can reach which endpoint, see [Role and Endpoint Access](./role-endpoint-access.mdx).

## Receipt Data Exposure {#receipt-data-exposure}

Receipt templates get the savings without reading `_woocommerce_pos_data` themselves:

- **Each line** — `regular_price`, `unit_savings` and `line_savings` give what a till price or a sale price saved. `discounts` is the line's `subtotal` − `total`: its share of coupons and **Add Discount**, never a till price.
- **`discounts[]`** — one row per coupon line, **Add Discount** included. An **Add Discount** row is labelled **Discount (10%)**, or **Discount** for a fixed amount, with `code` `pos-discount` and `discount_type` `percent` or `fixed_cart`.
- **Totals** — `discount_total` (coupons and **Add Discount**), `sale_savings_total` (regular-to-selling-price savings, till prices included), and `total_saved` for one figure. `total_saved` removes the overlap on orders from before v1.9.0.
- **`total_saved_complete`** — false when a line lacks the price data for a reliable sum; the savings totals are then `null`.

Each has `_incl` and `_excl` variants. The `_woocommerce_pos_data` meta itself is left out of `lines[].meta`. See [Displaying regular price and savings](../receipts/receipt-data.mdx#displaying-regular-price-and-savings) and [Totals](../receipts/receipt-data.mdx#totals) for template examples.

- **Check changes waiting to send.** See [Database](../support/store-health.mdx#database), or ["changes waiting to send" won't go down](../support/store-health.mdx#waiting-to-send).
- **Check what's downloaded.** See [What's on this device](../support/store-health.mdx#coverage).
- **When does a change made in WooCommerce reach the till?** See [How the POS learns about changes](#change-signal).
- **A sale or edit didn't reach your store.** See [Changes that never reached your server](../support/store-health.mdx#refused-changes).
- **"The POS is open in another tab."** See [One tab runs the POS](#multi-tab).
- **Updating to 2.0.** See [Before you click Update](../getting-started/upgrading-to-2.mdx#before-you-click-update).

:::warning Before updating to 2.0
On every till, open **Store health → Database** and wait until **changes waiting to send** reads 0, then update. Changes still waiting on a till are not sent after the update. See [Upgrading to 2.0](#upgrading).
:::

WCPOS is local-first. Every screen reads and writes a database on the device, and a **sync engine** keeps that database and your WooCommerce store in step in the background. The rest of this page explains what the engine fetches, when, and how sales get back to your store, for developers, integrators and store owners who want to know what the POS asks of their hosting.

## One database per store and cashier {#one-engine-per-scope}

Each store and cashier on a device has its own local database. Two cashiers on one device never share local data.

- **Switching is all or nothing.** The new store or cashier loads its database first and only takes over once it is ready. An error is shown, never a half-switched till.
- **Every request names its store.** On WCPOS Pro with several stores, a price changed at the till updates that store's price, not the web store's.
- **A new version starts a new database.** Nothing is migrated in place. See [Upgrading to 2.0](#upgrading).

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="How engines and scopes are arranged">

Each device runs **one sync engine per site**. Inside it, every store and cashier pair is a **scope** with its own local database. Switching store or cashier switches the scope, and its whole database, on the same engine. Connecting to a different site replaces the engine.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## What runs in the background {#sync-lanes}

The till's **change check** asks your store whether anything changed, every 60 seconds on **Balanced**. The [sync presets](../support/store-health.mdx#sync-presets) set it from every 10 seconds (**Realtime**) to every 5 minutes (**Eco**).

- **Background work waits for the cashier.** Top-ups of products, customers and variations only run after 60 seconds with no one using the till and no screen waiting for data, and they hold off while your server is under pressure.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="Every lane, its cadence and its request ceiling">

Everything the engine does on a timer is a **lane**: a named, bounded piece of background work. The change check is the one you can tune. Changes made on the device go out about 0.3 seconds after they are made.

Seeds, trickles, totals and audits each declare how many requests one run may make. The three paths a cashier waits on (the change check, the write drain and the demand drain) don't have a ceiling yet. The budgets are in [Sync Performance](sync-performance.mdx).

| Lane | Cadence | Requests per run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change check | Your preset: 10 s (Realtime), 60 s (Balanced), 5 min (Eco); custom from 5 s | No ceiling yet |
| Write drain | About 0.3 s after each change, 10 s backstop | No ceiling yet |
| Demand drain (fetches what screens declared) | 30 s | No ceiling yet |
| Recent orders, product and reference seeds | 5 min | 0 (they queue work for the demand drain) |
| Customer, product and variation trickles | 5 min, after 60 s idle | 1 |
| Server totals retry | 30 s | 11 |
| Coverage compaction | 60 s | 0 |
| Existence prime | 15 min | 5 |
| Deletion audit | 17 min | 11 |

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## How the POS learns about changes {#change-signal}

The POS doesn't download your catalog again to spot changes. At each change check it asks your store one light question: has anything moved since last time? On **Balanced**, a connected till checks for price and stock changes about once a minute. Checks can take longer when your server is under pressure. Orders follow their own, separate schedule.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="How the change check works">

Your store keeps a **change log**, and the POS asks whether anything moved since its last position in it.

- **One check covers eight kinds of record:** products, variations, tax rates, customers, coupons, categories, brands and tags. Orders are not on it; open and recent orders have their own lane, so product and order updates arrive on different rhythms.
- **A quiet store costs almost nothing.** The request is conditional. When nothing changed, your server answers `304 Not Modified` with no body.
- **Checks are spread out by ±20%,** so several tills on one site drift apart instead of asking at the same moment.
- **An unused till slows down, a little.** After 10 minutes with no touch, key press or accepted scan, checks stretch to at least 30 seconds, then at least 60. A hidden window goes straight to 60. On **Balanced** and **Eco** that changes nothing. The next touch brings the full rate back with an immediate check, unless your server has asked the POS to back off. The idle rate is never faster than your setting.
- **A till that was closed for days starts from now.** If more than 5,000 changes piled up, or your store pruned its log past the till's position, the engine jumps to the head of the log and re-checks what the device already holds. The cost follows the size of the local copy, not the time away.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## How screens get their data {#declared-demand}

A screen always shows what is already on the device, straight away. When it needs records the device doesn't have yet, such as a search past the first products, the engine fetches them from your store while the till is online. Not everything is on the device from the start; what is depends on the kind of record:

1. **Tax rates** are fetched at start-up, first: the cart can't do its sums without them.
2. **Products:** the first 100 in the default order are fetched at start-up. The rest arrive when a screen asks for them, and through an idle top-up in the cashier's sort order.
3. **Customers** are not fetched at start-up. A screen asks for them, an idle top-up adds 10 at a time, and the change check brings new and edited ones.
4. **Categories, tags, brands and coupons** are not fetched at start-up either. They load when first opened (a reopen within 15 seconds doesn't ask again), are filled in the background once your store reports it has some, are refreshed every 30 minutes and follow the change check.

When the till is online, opening the variation picker refreshes its prices and stock. Variations already on the device show at once while it does.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="How a screen asks for data">

Screens don't sync. **A screen declares what it shows (search, filters, sort, page) and the engine decides whether that needs a request.** Each declaration ends one of three ways:

- **Served locally** — every record it needs is already on the device.
- **Fetched** — the engine went to your store for it.
- **Released** — the screen let it go, usually because you scrolled or changed a filter and a newer declaration replaced it. This is routine, not an error.

A filter your store can't express is applied on the device; the rest of the query may still go to your store. A declaration that resolves doesn't mean a kind of record is fully downloaded. That is tracked separately, [below](#coverage).

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Is everything downloaded? {#coverage}

**Store health → Database** lists each kind of record with the count on the device, your store's total and a coverage bar. See [What's on this device](../support/store-health.mdx#coverage). A number there is never made up from what the device holds:

- **A total that is out of date or missing reads *checking…*.** Out of date, the last figure from your store stays beside it. A count from the device would always read 100% and hide the very gap the figure is there to show.
- **Orders read partial by design.** The bar measures against every order in your store, while the till keeps only open and recent ones (**open + recent stay on device · older download when viewed**).

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="How download totals are checked">

A figure like *1,240 of 5,000 products* needs your store's total for the second number. The engine keeps one per kind of record. Each is a cheap request of its own, one record per page, reading WooCommerce's `X-WP-Total` header, at most once every 15 minutes or so per kind.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## How changes get back to your store {#write-path}

Every change made on the device (a sale, a product edit, a new customer) is saved on the device first and sent to your store within a second or so. **A sale rung up offline waits on the device and goes when the connection comes back.** Most changes need nothing from you. Two kinds wait for a choice on **Store health → Database**:

- **Someone changed it first, on another till or in WooCommerce.** The change is held instead of going over theirs. **Send again** sends this till's change on top of your store's version, so compare the two first. **Use server version** drops this till's change and puts your store's version back on the till. See [Changes held because your store had a newer version](../support/store-health.mdx#held-changes).
- **Your store refused it and gave a reason**, listed as **changes never reached your server**. It is never retried on its own. Correct the record if the reason asks for it, then **Send again**. **Discard** drops this till's change and keeps your store's version. See [Changes that never reached your server](../support/store-health.mdx#refused-changes).

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="How changes are sent">

- **A durable queue comes first.** Each change goes into a queue on the device before anything is sent. About 0.3 seconds later the write drain sends it; a 10-second poll catches anything a nudge missed.
- **Cart changes go one at a time per order.** A fast run of scans applies in order. Adding a product that already has exactly one line in the cart raises that line's quantity instead of adding a second line.
- **An order that clashes is retried once before it is held.** If your store has a newer copy of an order, the engine reconciles the two and sends once more. Only if that fails too, or the record is not an order, is the change held.
- **Send again on a refused change rebuilds it** from the record as it is on the device now, so a correction you made is what gets sent.
- **What your store sends back is kept.** When your store accepts a change, the device takes your store's copy, so later edits match it. For orders that keeps the line IDs WooCommerce assigned, so an update doesn't add duplicate lines. A local edit your store hasn't seen yet is never overwritten.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## One tab runs the POS {#multi-tab}

In a web browser, **only one tab per site runs the POS**. A second tab of the same site, for any store or cashier, stops at this screen. It never opens the local database, so it can't ring a sale or send anything, and nothing in the first tab is disturbed.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={5} offsets={[[637, 37], [334, 0]]} alt="A second browser tab of the POS: The POS is open in another tab, Only one tab can run the register, and the Take over here button" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **The POS is open in another tab** — this tab is parked. **Only one tab can run the register.**
2. **Take over here** — asks the other tab to hand over. It finishes a payment in progress and waits up to 15 seconds for a save, then parks itself, and this tab loads the POS.

</div>

- **A parked tab doesn't wake up by itself.** Closing the live tab leaves the other one parked until you press **Take over here** or reload it.
- **"The other tab isn't answering"** — the live tab didn't hand over. Close it, or reload it, to carry on in this one.
- **A payment about to start during a handover** is refused before it is sent, and logged as [CLIENT161](../error-codes/CLIENT161.mdx). A payment already under way finishes first.
- **Desktop and mobile apps** have no tabs and no gate. A browser without the Web Locks API runs every tab without the check.

## Local storage {#local-storage}

In 2.0 every platform keeps its data in a **SQLite** database on the device.

If the storage worker is lost, or a local save stalls, the POS stops anything that could lose a sale. A banner reads **Local database unavailable — scanning, checkout, saving and voiding are blocked until the app is reloaded.** Refunds are blocked too, and a scan shows **Scanning unavailable** instead of going quiet. Press **Reload the app**. The code is [SYNC161](../error-codes/SYNC161.mdx).

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="Where the data lives on each platform">

The database runs through RxDB Premium:

| Platform | Where the data lives |
| --- | --- |
| Web | SQLite-wasm in one dedicated worker, in the browser's private file system (OPFS) |
| Desktop | SQLite in the app's main process |
| iOS and Android | SQLite through expo-sqlite |

Queries run inside the database: filter, sort and page. Only the rows on screen reach the app.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Upgrading to 2.0 {#upgrading}

2.0 doesn't migrate local data. It does a **cold resync**:

1. **Every device starts a fresh database** and downloads again from your store, once. Your store holds the full copy of everything synced.
2. **Changes queued before the update are not sent.** On every till, wait until **changes waiting to send** reads 0 on **Store health → Database** before you update. See [Before you click Update](../getting-started/upgrading-to-2.mdx#before-you-click-update). The old 1.x databases are deleted once the store is ready to sell.
3. **The app and the plugin go together.** The 2.0 app needs the WCPOS 2.0 plugin. With only one side updated, the app says which: [AUTH331](../error-codes/AUTH331.mdx) when the plugin is older, [SYNC341](../error-codes/SYNC341.mdx) when the app is older.

## What changed since 1.9 {#what-changed-in-v1100}

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem question="1.9, 1.10–1.11 and 2.0 side by side">

| | 1.9 | 1.10–1.11 | 2.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit of sync | One stream per screen query | One engine, a database per site, store and cashier | Same |
| What drives a fetch | A screen opening | A screen declaring what it needs | Same |
| Change detection | Periodic polls and an hourly full audit | Change log with conditional `304` answers | Same |
| Your store's totals | Not tracked | Per kind of record, with *checking…* | Same |
| Refused changes | Retried out of sight | Durable queue, recovery panel, no silent retries | Same |
| Browser tabs | Not coordinated | Every tab live, one elected sender | One live tab per site, others parked |
| Storage | IndexedDB (web), SQLite (apps) | File-system storage (OPFS on web) | SQLite on every platform |
| Sync tuning | Fixed | Presets and sliders in Store health | Same |

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

Most barcode scanners need no setup: plug the scanner in, or pair it like a keyboard, and scan. The wizard below asks a few questions and takes you through the rest, or through the fix when a scan doesn't come through.

Most fixes happen on one screen, **Settings → Barcode Scanning**. Below the wizard: [what that screen holds](#barcode-scanning-screen), [how to read the test panel](#test-panel) and [what your scanner's status means](#scanner-status).

  <WizardStep id="kb-first-d" title="Start in keyboard mode — no configuration needed">

Out of the box, nearly every scanner acts as a **keyboard**: it "types" each barcode. WCPOS recognises the fast keystrokes as a scan. There is nothing to configure.

1. **USB scanner:** plug it in — that's it.
2. **Bluetooth scanner:** put it in pairing mode (check the manual — usually holding the trigger or a beep sequence) and pair it in your **system Bluetooth settings**, like any keyboard.
3. Open WCPOS and scan any product barcode. The product should appear and be added to the sale.

  <WizardFix id="fix-nothing" title="When scanning does nothing">

1. **Does the scanner react?** Pull the trigger — no beep or light means power/battery, not WCPOS.
2. **Is it connected?** Bluetooth settings must say **Connected**, not just "paired". Re-pair if unsure.
3. **Prove it types:** open any notes app or an empty text field and scan. If characters appear there, the scanner is fine — the POS just isn't recognising the input as a scan.
4. If it types but WCPOS ignores it: open <kbd>Settings</kbd> → <kbd>Barcode Scanning</kbd> and scan into the **Barcode Scanning Test** panel. It says why each attempt was treated as typing and, for a scanner that is only a little slow, offers to raise the threshold with an **Apply** button. See [Check a scan in the test panel](#test-panel).

  <WizardFix id="fix-garbled-d" title="When scans come out garbled">

A keyboard-mode scanner "types" using a **keyboard layout**. If the scanner's layout doesn't match your device's (QWERTY vs AZERTY, symbol positions), characters swap.

1. Scan into the **Barcode Scanning Test** panel (<kbd>Settings</kbd> → <kbd>Barcode Scanning</kbd>). It spots a scanner that sends Shift as separate keys and tells you what it sees. See [Check a scan in the test panel](#test-panel).
2. Check your scanner manual for **"keyboard language"** or **"keyboard layout"** setup barcodes and set it to match your device (US layout is the usual safe choice).
3. The most robust fix: a **direct connection**. A directly connected scanner sends the barcode as data — no keyboard layout involved, so nothing can garble.

  <WizardFix id="fix-focus-d" title="Scans in the wrong place, or lost without focus">

The real fix on this platform is a **direct connection**: every scan is delivered straight to WCPOS, whichever field has focus.

  <WizardFix id="fix-osk-a" title="Getting the on-screen keyboard back">

Phones and tablets hide the on-screen keyboard while a hardware keyboard is connected — and a keyboard-mode scanner *is* a hardware keyboard. This is the operating system's behaviour, not a WCPOS setting.

1. **Most scanners have a shortcut to toggle it back** — commonly holding the trigger or a small function button for a few seconds (Inateck: hold the scan button ~3&nbsp;s). Look for *"iOS keyboard"* or *"virtual keyboard"* in your scanner's manual.
2. Or disconnect the scanner in Bluetooth settings when you need to type a lot.

  <WizardStep id="android-register" title="Register your scanner — no mode switch needed">

On Android, WCPOS identifies your scanner by its **device identity**, so you can leave it in its factory keyboard mode. No setup barcodes, no re-pairing.

1. Pair the scanner in Android's Bluetooth settings (or plug it in over USB) — exactly as it came out of the box.
2. In WCPOS open <kbd>Settings</kbd> → <kbd>Barcode Scanning</kbd>. In the **Scanner** section tap **Register a scanner**, then <kbd>Register scanner</kbd>.
3. Scan **any barcode**. WCPOS detects which device it came from — "Detected *NETUM Bluetooth* — name it and save".
4. Give it a name (e.g. "Front counter") and tap <kbd>Save scanner</kbd>.

From now on every scan from that scanner goes straight to WCPOS — no typing-speed guesswork, and scans aren't lost when no field is focused.

  <WizardFix id="fix-android" title="When registration doesn't detect the scanner">

1. Registration listens for keyboard input, so the scanner must be in its **factory keyboard mode**. If it was ever switched to SPP/BLE mode for another app, scan the **"HID mode"** / **"keyboard mode"** barcode in its manual to restore the default, then forget &amp; re-pair it in Bluetooth settings.
2. Bluetooth settings must show **Connected** — not just paired.
3. Prove it types: open any notes app and scan. No characters there means the scanner isn't delivering input at all (battery, pairing).

  <WizardStep id="mode-netum" title="Switch your Netum into serial (SPP) mode">

Your Netum ships in keyboard (HID) mode. To connect it directly, switch its mode with a barcode from **your model's own manual**:

:::info In the manual, find the section and scan the barcode named:
**`Bluetooth SPP Mode`**

Section: **"Bluetooth Connection Modes"** — Netum's manuals for every model are at [netum.net → Barcode Scanner User Manuals](https://www.netum.net/pages/barcode-scanner-user-manuals). Netum's own description of SPP mode: *"If you have an application that supports our scanner, this is the recommended mode."*
:::

:::warning Forget, then pair again
After switching modes, your device still remembers the old keyboard pairing. Open your **system Bluetooth settings**, forget/remove the scanner, then pair it again. Skipping this is the most common reason the next step fails.
:::

**Connecting over USB instead?** In **"USB Connection Modes"**, scan **`USB COM Port Emulation`** or **`USB Virtual COM`**.

**To undo at any time:** scan the **Bluetooth HID Mode** barcode in the same section — that restores the factory keyboard mode. (Netum's sequence: forget the scanner in Bluetooth settings → Bluetooth off → scan the barcode → Bluetooth on → pair again.)

## The Barcode Scanning screen {#barcode-scanning-screen}

Open the menu, then <kbd>Settings</kbd> → <kbd>Barcode Scanning</kbd>. Changes save as you make them; there is no Save button.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={1} offsets={[[60, 0], [60, 0], [270, 0], [160, 0], [-30, 0], [20, 0]]} alt="The Barcode Scanning screen: the timing threshold and minimum length, the Scanner section with its setup guide link and the direct connection row, and the Barcode Scanning Test panel" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Barcode Average Time Input Threshold (ms)** — how fast keys must arrive to count as a scan. The default is 24. Raise it for a slower scanner; the [test panel](#test-panel) tells you by how much.
2. **Barcode Minimum Length** — a scan shorter than this is skipped at the till, never added to the cart. The default is 8.
3. **No setup needed for most scanners** — shows until you register or connect a scanner. A keyboard-mode scanner works without any row here.
4. **Scanner setup guide** — opens this page.
5. **Connect a scanner directly (advanced)** — opens the [direct connection sheet](#direct-connection-sheet). On Android this row reads **Register a scanner** instead; see [the wizard](#wizard).
6. **Barcode Scanning Test** — scan into this panel to see what WCPOS makes of it. See [Check a scan in the test panel](#test-panel).

</div>

Between them, **Barcode Scanner Prefix** and **Barcode Scanner Suffix** each take one character your scanner adds before or after every code, so WCPOS strips it before it searches. **Play a sound on scan** turns the scan sound on or off. For each setting in detail, see [Barcode Settings](../../settings/store/barcode.mdx#settings).

## Check a scan in the test panel {#test-panel}

Scan any barcode while the Barcode Scanning screen is open. The panel shows the keys the scanner sent and how WCPOS read them. If your latest attempt is marked **scan** in Recent attempts and **Detected Barcode** matches the code printed on the product, go straight to [Finish at the till](#finish-at-the-till).

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={2} offsets={[[200, 0], [200, 0], [420, 0], [340, -25], [300, 0]]} alt="The test panel after a clean scan: the keys received, the detected barcode, a timing chart with every bar under the dashed threshold line, the verdict, and the list of recent attempts" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Keypress Event** — every key the scanner sent. <kbd>↵</kbd> is Enter and <kbd>⇧</kbd> is Shift.
2. **Detected Barcode** — the code WCPOS would search for.
3. **Timing chart** — one bar per key, showing the gap before it in milliseconds. The dashed line is your threshold; a bar above it turns amber.
4. **Verdict** — scan or typing, with the average gap between keys.
5. **Recent attempts** — your last five, newest first, each marked **scan**, **too short** or **typing** with its average gap.

</div>

Each attempt ends one of five ways:

- **Detected as a scan, marked scan.** The keys came fast enough and the code is at least your minimum length. Go to [Finish at the till](#finish-at-the-till).
- **Detected as a scan, marked too short.** The keys came fast enough, but the code is shorter than **Barcode Minimum Length**, so the till skips it. Tap **Apply** on **Lower minimum length**.
- **Treated as typing, in amber, "above your threshold".** A near miss: the keys came fast, but not fast enough for your threshold. See [If the scan is too slow](#too-slow).
- **Treated as typing … correctly ignored.** The keys came at typing speed. Nothing to fix.
- **Treated as typing — only one key pressed.** One key gives no gaps to measure. Scan a whole barcode.

A pause of more than a second starts a new attempt in the list.

### If the scan is too slow {#too-slow}

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={3} offsets={[[420, 0], [460, 12], [300, -14]]} alt="The test panel after a slow scan: amber bars above the dashed threshold line, the near-miss verdict with an offer to raise the threshold, and the attempt marked typing" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Amber bars** — the gaps that were slower than your threshold.
2. **Near miss** — the average gap, and an offer to raise the threshold to just above it. **Apply** saves the new threshold at once.
3. **Recent attempts** — the slow scan is marked **typing**.

</div>

### If extra characters appear {#extra-characters}

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={4} offsets={[[0, -32], [330, -16], [300, -39]]} alt="The test panel after a scan from a scanner that sends Shift as separate keys and puts a symbol before the code: the Shift warning and an offer to set the symbol as the prefix" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Timing chart** — the <kbd>⇧</kbd> bars are Shift keys the scanner sent on their own.
2. **Suggestions** — strip the leading symbol by setting it as your prefix, and a warning that the scanner sends Shift as separate keys.
3. **Recent attempts** — this one still counts as a scan.

</div>

Every suggestion comes with an **Apply** button that saves the change straight away:

- **Raise threshold to … ms** — your scanner is a little slower than the threshold.
- **Lower minimum length to …** — the code is shorter than your minimum length.
- **Strip leading "…" — set it as your prefix** — the scan starts with a symbol and you have no prefix set.

Two notes come without a button. **Scanner is sending Shift as separate keys** means you should set the scanner to a US keyboard, no-modifier mode with its manual's setup barcodes. **Trailing Enter detected and ignored — no suffix needed** means the Enter at the end of a scan is already handled, so leave the suffix empty.

**After any Apply**, scan the same barcode again.

### Finish at the till {#finish-at-the-till}

When your latest attempt is marked **scan** in Recent attempts, not **too short** or **typing**, and **Detected Barcode** matches the code printed on the product, open the register and scan a simple product you know. It goes straight into the cart. In the browser or the desktop app, click a blank part of the screen first: while a text field has the cursor, the search box included, WCPOS leaves the keys there as typing.

If the scan reaches the till but nothing is added, for example **Barcode not found**, see [Scan a barcode](../../pos/product-panel/index.mdx#scan) for what each message means.

## Read your scanner's status {#scanner-status}

Only a scanner you register on Android, or connect directly, gets its own row in the **Scanner** section. A keyboard-mode scanner you haven't registered has no row and needs none, so you can skip this part.

3. **<kbd>⋮</kbd> menu** — **Remove** deletes the row.

| Status | Kind of scanner | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Connected** | Any direct connection | Connected now. |
| **Registered** | Keyboard mode, registered on Android | Saved. Its scans are recognised by device. |
| **Not in range** | Bluetooth | Saved, but not connected now. |
| **Not plugged in** | USB serial | Saved, but not connected now. |
| **Not connected** | USB HID POS | Saved, but not connected now. |

When you open WCPOS, a saved scanner reconnects by itself if exactly one allowed device matches it. With two identical scanners, connect each one again from the sheet.

### The direct connection sheet {#direct-connection-sheet}

**Connect a scanner directly (advanced)** opens this sheet. Use it only after you have switched the scanner out of keyboard mode; [the wizard](#wizard) walks you through that.

<details>
<summary>Connect over USB or Bluetooth serial, or add a Bluetooth service UUID</summary>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={6} offsets={[[-26, 0], [211, 0], [-26, 0], [380, 0], [0, 34], [240, 0]]} alt="The direct connection sheet: Connect over USB, Connect over Bluetooth serial, the note about keyboard-mode scanners, and the field to add a scanner's Bluetooth service UUID" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Connect over USB** — for a scanner in USB HID POS mode. While it is connected, the button reads **Disconnect USB scanner**.
2. **Connect over Bluetooth serial** — for a scanner in SPP mode, or in USB COM mode on a cable: the chooser lists USB serial ports too. While it is connected, the button reads **Disconnect Bluetooth serial scanner**.
3. **Keyboard-mode note** — a scanner in keyboard mode can't be connected here. That is a rule of the operating system, not of WCPOS.

5. **Add** — saves the UUID so the chooser can find the scanner.

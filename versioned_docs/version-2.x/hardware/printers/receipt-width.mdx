A receipt printer fits a set number of characters on each line: 32 on 58 mm paper, and 42, 48 or 64 on 80 mm paper. The app lays out every receipt to the number you pick. Pick too many and the long lines wrap onto a second line; pick too few and the prices stop short of the right edge. The test page has a ruler that tells you which way you're off.

Already added your printer? See [Change the width later](#change-later).

## Print a test page {#answer}

{/* reshoot: the Paper width toggle, Did the test page print? and Which paper is in the printer? screens need a receipt printer the capture browser can reach */}

In **Add printer**, after you pick your printer:

1. **Set the paper, if asked.** If **Paper width** appears, pick the roll in your printer: **58 mm** or **80 mm**.
2. **Press Print a test page.**
3. **Check the ruler** in section **1 COLUMN RULER** of the printout. See [Read the ruler](#what-the-ruler-is-off-means) below.
4. **Answer Did the test page print?**
   - **Yes, and it looks right** — saves the printer at the width it just printed.
   - **Printed, but the ruler is off** — the app asks **Which paper is in the printer?** and lists the four widths.
     1. Choose a width, using [Which width to pick](#which-answer-to-pick). The app prints the test page again at that width.
     2. Check the new ruler.
     3. Still off? Choose again. When the ruler ends at the right edge of the printing, answer **Yes, and it looks right**.
   - **Nothing came out** — this isn't a width problem. See [When nothing prints](./index.mdx#when-nothing-prints).

The line under the answers counts your test pages and gives the width of the last one, for example **Test page 2 · 42 characters per line**.

For a printer you've already saved, press **Test Print** on its row in **Settings → Printing**.

<details>
<summary>When Paper width appears, and the size it starts on</summary>

The mobile apps, and the desktop app for a USB printer, first ask the printer for its paper size and show **Checking paper width…**. **Paper width** appears only when the app still can't tell. It starts on 80 mm for Epson and Star printers. For any other printer, make sure it shows your roll before you print.

</details>

## Which width to pick {#which-answer-to-pick}

| Answer | Pick it when |
|---|---|
| **32 — 58 mm paper** | The narrow roll: most small, portable and Bluetooth printers |
| **48 — 80 mm, most Epson & Star** | Your first try on 80 mm paper |
| **42 — 80 mm, older printers** | 48 wrapped onto a second line |
| **64 — 80 mm, small font** | Star printers on the small font, when 48 stops short. See [Font A and Font B](#font-a-and-font-b). |

Not sure which roll you have? Measure across it: 58 mm paper is just under 6 cm wide, 80 mm paper is 8 cm.

## Change the width later {#change-later}

1. **Open the printer.** Go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Edit** on the printer's row.
2. **Choose the number under Paper width:** **32**, **42**, **48** or **64**. Here the setting is called **Paper width**, but it is the same characters-per-line list as **Printer text width**, not the 58 mm / 80 mm question from setup. The change saves as you make it.
3. **Press Test Print**, at the bottom of the same dialog or on the printer's row, and [read the ruler](#what-the-ruler-is-off-means) again.

## If something goes wrong {#troubleshooting}

**Lines of the receipt wrap onto a second line.** The width is too big for the paper. Go one step down: 48 to 42 on 80 mm paper, or anything to 32 on 58 mm paper.

**The prices stop short of the right edge.** The width is too small. Go one step up: 42 to 48. On a Star printer where 48 already stops short, see [Font A and Font B](#font-a-and-font-b).

**The logo is cut off on 58 mm paper.** Set the width to **32**, and print with a receipt template made for 58 mm paper, such as **Simple Thermal Receipt (58mm)**, or one that sets no paper size. A template made for 80 mm paper sizes the logo for 80 mm whatever the width. The test page's logo always follows the width, so it can print whole while receipts are still cut off.

**£, € or accented letters print as other characters.** That's the printer's character table, not the width: look at section **8 CHARACTERS** on the test page. See [Non-Latin receipts](./non-latin-receipts.mdx).

## Font A and Font B {#font-a-and-font-b}

Receipt printers have two built-in fonts. Font A is the standard one. Font B is smaller and fits more characters on a line, which is what **64** is for. On 80 mm paper, use 48 or 42 for an ESC/POS printer. Use 64 only when a Star printer is already set to its smaller font.

<details>
<summary>How your printer's font affects the width</summary>

- **ESC/POS printers** (**Printer Language** set to **ESC/POS**, as on most Epson printers): the app asks for Font A at the start of every test page and every receipt. A printer left on Font B in its own setup still prints Font A from the app, so stay with 48 or 42.
- **Star printers** (**StarPRNT** or **Star Line Mode**): the app prints in whatever font the printer is set to. If 48 stops well short on 80 mm paper, the printer is on its small font. Pick **64**, or set the printer back to Font A in Star's own setup tool and keep 48.

</details>

## Still stuck? {#still-stuck}

Press **Copy setup report** and paste it into your message. It's on the **Nothing printed** screen in **Add printer**, and in the **More actions** menu on the printer's row in **Settings → Printing**. The report records the printer, the width the app is using and the app's recent printer log, which shows how the printer answered. From **Add printer** it also records how many test pages printed and the last error. In the mobile apps it opens the share sheet instead of copying.

A cash drawer doesn't connect to the POS. It plugs into the **receipt printer**, and the POS asks the printer to open it. This page wires the drawer, turns it on in the app, and tests it.

:::note You need a receipt printer first
Add the printer before the drawer. See [Printer Setup Wizard](./setup-wizard.mdx). The built-in **Print Dialog** and cloud printers can't open a drawer.
:::

## Plug the drawer into the printer {#wire-it-up}

1. Find the socket on the back of the printer marked **DK**, **Drawer** or **Cash Drawer**.
2. Plug in the drawer's cable. Its plug looks like a telephone plug. The network socket beside it is wider: don't use that one.
3. Switch the printer off and on.

## Turn it on in the app {#switch-it-on-in-the-app}

The switch is **Auto-open cash drawer**, one per printer. It's off for a new printer. You can turn it on while you add the printer, or later from the printer's row.

**While you add the printer:** in **Settings → Printing**, press **Add printer**. When the search finishes, press **More options**.

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **More options** — opens the printer's settings under the search results. Check **Vendor** here too: **Epson** or **Star Micronics**. Each make has its own drawer command, so pick the make of your printer.
2. **Auto-open cash drawer** — the printer opens the drawer each time it prints a receipt.
3. **Set as default** — **No sale** opens the drawer on the default printer. See [Open the drawer without a sale](#no-sale).

</div>

**On a printer you already have:** in **Settings → Printing**, press **Edit** on the printer's row. **Edit** has the same switch, and two more controls:

- **Cash drawer connector** — the pin the drawer is wired to. **Pin 2 / Drawer 1** suits most drawers. Pick **Pin 5 / Drawer 2** for a second drawer, or when the drawer's manual says pin 5. It shows while **Auto-open cash drawer** is on.
- **Open drawer** — at the foot of **Edit**, beside **Test Print**. It opens the drawer once, without printing, so you can test the wiring. **Cash drawer opened** means the printer took the command.

:::tip Test with Open drawer
The setup wizard's test page never opens the drawer. Once the printer is saved, test the drawer with **Open drawer** in **Edit**.
:::

## When the drawer opens {#when-it-opens}

With **Auto-open cash drawer** on, the drawer opens whenever that printer prints a receipt:

- a receipt printed at checkout, by hand or automatically;
- a receipt you print again later;
- **Test Print** on the printer's row.

It opens for every payment method, not only cash.

It stays shut for register reports: the X-report, the handover and the closure. See [Open the Register](../../pos/register/index.mdx).

## Open the drawer without a sale {#no-sale}

To make change or check the float, use **No sale** in the register panel. It opens the drawer and records why, with no money moving.

Tap your avatar at the end of the register bar, then **In the drawer**, to open the register panel.

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **No sale** — opens the drawer. Only while the register is open. **Paid in** and **Paid out** beside it record cash going in or out, and don't open the drawer.
2. **In the drawer** — the cash the register expects. A No sale doesn't change it. **Print X-report** at the foot never opens the drawer.

</div>

Press **No sale**. The sheet asks **Why open the drawer?**

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={3} offsets={[[-30, 0], [440, 0], [165, 0]]} alt="The No sale sheet, Why open the drawer?: the cash in the drawer, the reasons Change, Mistake, Drawer check and Other…, the reason field with Change filled in, and the Open the drawer button" />

<div className="pinned-legend">

1. **Change** — one of the ready reasons, with **Mistake** and **Drawer check**. Tap one to fill in the reason. **Other…** clears the field so you can type your own.
2. **Reason** — the reason that's recorded. Until there is one, the sheet says **Add a reason**.
3. **Open the drawer** — records the No sale and opens the drawer.

## If the drawer doesn't open {#when-it-doesnt-open}

Start with **Open drawer** in **Edit**. It tells you whether the problem is the wiring or the settings.

**Open drawer works, but receipts don't open the drawer.** The wiring is right. Check that **Auto-open cash drawer** is on for the printer that prints your receipts.

**Open drawer shows "The printer did not accept the job."** The printer didn't take the command. Print a test page from the printer's row first. See [Receipt Printers](./index.mdx#when-nothing-prints).

**The app says Cash drawer opened, but nothing happens.** Check the cable is in the drawer socket, not the network socket. Then try the other **Cash drawer connector**: the drawer may be wired to pin 5.

**The drawer clicks but doesn't open.** Check the drawer's key. Most drawers have a lock on the front, and one key position keeps the drawer shut. Then check the voltage: drawers are made for 12 V or 24 V, and the drawer has to match what the printer puts out. The printer's manual says which.

**"Cash drawer kicks depend on printer language/emulation."** This warning appears in **Edit** when the **Printer Language** doesn't match the printer's make, for example a Star printer set to ESC/POS. Set the language to match the printer's emulation mode.

**No sale closed, but the drawer stayed shut.** The No sale is recorded either way. Check that your default receipt printer has **Auto-open cash drawer** on. If the printer refused, the sheet stays open with the error: press **Open the drawer** again. That only opens the drawer, it doesn't record a second No sale.

**A low-cost printer doesn't respond.** Some printers have no drawer socket, or don't drive one. The printer's manual says whether it supports a cash drawer.

## Still stuck? {#still-stuck}

On the printer's row in **Settings → Printing**, open the menu and press **Copy setup report**. Paste it into your message, with the printer and drawer models.

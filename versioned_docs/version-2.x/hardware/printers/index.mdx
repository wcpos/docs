Printers live under **Settings → Printing** in the POS. Each device keeps its own list: a printer added on one till doesn't appear on another, so set it up on every device that prints to it.

With no printer added, receipts still print through **Print Dialog**, the browser's or computer's own print window. It's built in and always there.

:::tip Printer in another room, or shared by every till?
Use [Cloud Printing](../../receipts/cloud-printing.mdx). Cloud printers appear on every device by themselves; you don't add them here.
:::

## Add a printer {#adding-a-printer}

1. Go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Add printer**. The window reads **Looking for printers…** and the search starts straight away; there is nothing to choose first.
2. Find your printer. Each one the search finds is a card, under **Found your printer** for one or **Which printer is yours?** for several. In the web app the search only covers Wi-Fi: for a USB or Bluetooth printer, press **Find a USB printer** or **Find a Bluetooth printer** instead (see [When the scan finds nothing](#nothing-found)).
3. Press **Print a test page** on its card.
4. Answer **Did the test page print?** The printer is saved when you answer **Yes, and it looks right**. See [Print a test page](#test-page).

If the card isn't your printer, press **Not this one? Scan again**. **Stop** ends the search early and keeps the printers already found; **Close** leaves without adding anything. If nothing turns up, see [When the scan finds nothing](#nothing-found).

The automatic search checks these connections, depending on the app:

- **Web app** — Wi-Fi.
- **Desktop app** — USB, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.
- **Android** — Wi-Fi; Epson and Star printers on Bluetooth and USB; other makes on Bluetooth Low Energy; and Bluetooth printers already paired in Android's settings.
- **iPhone and iPad** — Wi-Fi; Epson and Star printers on Bluetooth; other makes on Bluetooth Low Energy. There is no USB on iPhone and iPad.

### Print a test page {#test-page}

The test page is a numbered ruler across the paper, so it shows the width is right as well as that the printer works. If the app can't tell how wide the paper is, a **Paper width** switch, **58 mm** or **80 mm**, sits beside the card, already set to the likelier one. Check it before you print.

Then it asks **Did the test page print?**

- **Yes, and it looks right** — the printer is saved, with a message such as *Receipt Printer is set up*. Its name, width and cash drawer setting are under its row in **Settings → Printing**.
- **Printed, but the ruler is off** — the app asks **Which paper is in the printer?** Pick the line that matches, such as **48 — 80 mm, most Epson & Star**, and the test page prints again. See [Getting the receipt width right](./receipt-width.mdx).
- **Nothing came out** — the window reads **Nothing printed**, with a line on the most likely cause. Fix what it names, then press **Try again**.

<details>
<summary>If nothing printed: the other choices</summary>

- **Save without testing** — keep the printer anyway, without a test page.
- **Open Bluetooth settings** — Android only, when a Bluetooth printer isn't paired yet. Pair it there, then scan again.
- **Start over**, **Copy setup report** and **Open the printer guide** — begin again, or gather what support needs.

</details>

Nothing is saved until you answer **Yes, and it looks right** or press **Save without testing**, so closing the window part-way leaves nothing half set-up in the list.

## Your printers {#printers-list}

Once a printer is added, **Settings → Printing** lists it under **Printers**, *Devices receipts can be sent to.* Each row shows the printer's name, its address and make (for example *192.168.1.100:9100 · Epson*), and a **Default** tag on the one receipts go to unless sent elsewhere. **Print Dialog** is always in the list as *Print dialog · built-in*, and cloud printers show their provider, such as *PrintNode · managed by WCPOS*.

Each row has three controls: **Test Print**, **Edit** and **More actions** (**⋯**). **Edit** opens the printer's settings; see [Change a printer's settings](#printer-options). Print Dialog and cloud printers have no **Edit**: cloud printers are changed in [Cloud Printing](../../receipts/cloud-printing.mdx).

### Test a printer {#test-print}

Press **Test Print** on its row. A message says **Printed on** the printer when it confirms the page came out, or **Sent to** the printer when it can only confirm the job left the till. If it fails, the message says what went wrong.

### Make a printer the default {#set-default}

Open **More actions** (**⋯**) on its row and press **Set Default**. It isn't shown on the current default, Print Dialog, or cloud printers.

### Delete a printer {#delete-printer}

Open **More actions** (**⋯**) on its row, press **Delete** and confirm **Delete Receipt Printer?** (with its own name). The printer is removed from this device only. Any template that was routed to it *prints with Auto instead* (see [Print routing](#print-routing)).

### Send a setup report to support {#setup-report}

Open **More actions** (**⋯**) on its row and press **Copy setup report**. It gathers the app version, the printer's settings and the latest printing log. In the web and desktop apps it's copied, ready to paste into a support message; in the mobile apps the share sheet opens so you can send it.

## Change a printer's settings {#printer-options}

Press **Edit** on the printer's row. **Edit Printer** opens as a panel from the right. There's no Save button: each change is saved as soon as the form is complete, and **Close** saves anything still waiting.

2. **Connection** — **Network**, **Bluetooth** or **USB**. Changing it clears the address.
3. **Vendor** — **Epson** or **Star Micronics** in the web app.
4. **IP Address** — with **Port** beside it. *Detected: Epson* under it means the app reached the printer there.

6. **Printer Language** — the command set the printer understands: **ESC/POS**, **StarPRNT** or **Star Line Mode**.

8. **Receipt language** — the character set for ESC/POS printers. **Automatic** suits most shops; see [non-Latin receipts](./non-latin-receipts.mdx).

If the vendor and the printer language don't match (a Star printer on ESC/POS, or another make on a Star language), a warning appears above the switches: *Cash drawer kicks depend on printer language/emulation.* Set **Printer Language** to the mode the printer is set to emulate, or the drawer may stay shut.

At the bottom of the window:

- **Open drawer** — opens the cash drawer through this printer now, whether **Auto-open cash drawer** is on or not. Use it to check the wiring and the connector pin. See [Connecting a cash drawer](./cash-drawer.mdx).
- **Test Print** — prints a test page with the settings as they are. If it fails, the reason appears in red in the window, with **Having trouble?** and **Support details**. Open **Support details** to see what the app tried; in the web and desktop apps, **Copy support details** copies it for a support message.
- **Close** — saves and closes.

## What each app can connect to {#supported-printers-by-platform}

The desktop and mobile apps reach Wi-Fi receipt printers that speak ESC/POS or one of Star's command languages, which is most of them; [Supported receipt printers](./supported-printers.mdx) says which models are tested. The web app only adds Epson and Star printers. For another make, use the desktop or mobile app, or a [cloud printer](../../receipts/cloud-printing.mdx).

<details>
<summary>Every app, over Wi-Fi, USB and Bluetooth</summary>

A browser can't open a direct connection to a printer the way an app can. Over Wi-Fi the web app reaches Epson and Star printers through the web server built into the printer, not raw port 9100.

| | Over Wi-Fi | Over USB and Bluetooth |
|---|---|---|
| **Web app** | Epson and Star, through the printer's own web port. On an HTTPS store, Chrome asks for permission to reach your local network the first time. | Epson and Star by USB or Bluetooth Low Energy, through the browser's own device chooser. Chrome and Edge only. |
| **Desktop app** | Wi-Fi receipt printers that speak ESC/POS, StarPRNT or Star Line Mode. It scans the network to find them. | USB, Bluetooth Low Energy, and printers already installed (Windows) or paired (Mac) in the operating system. |
| **iPhone and iPad** | Wi-Fi receipt printers that speak ESC/POS, StarPRNT or Star Line Mode. | Epson and Star through the makers' own software; other makes over Bluetooth Low Energy. No USB. |
| **Android** | Wi-Fi receipt printers that speak ESC/POS, StarPRNT or Star Line Mode. | Epson and Star through the makers' own software; other makes over Bluetooth Low Energy, or Bluetooth Classic once [paired in Settings](./bluetooth-pairing.mdx#android-pair-first). |

</details>

## Print routing {#print-routing}

When you print with more than one template, say a thermal receipt and an A4 invoice, routing decides which printer each one goes to. Three layers are checked in this order:

1. **On the receipt screen.** Under **Template** sits a **Printer** choice, set to **Auto** and showing what Auto picked, such as *Auto — Print Dialog*. Picking a printer here sends this receipt there, and reprints go there too until you change template or leave the screen. Changing template sets it back to **Auto**.
2. **In Settings.** Under the printer list, **Receipt templates**, **Report templates** and **Closure templates** each list your active templates: *Choose which printer each template prints to.* Pick a printer for a template, or set it back to **Auto**. Cloud printers are in each list too.
3. **Auto.** With no choice made, the app sends each template to a printer that suits it, and to the default printer when several do.

Routing choices are kept on the device, like the printers themselves.

<details>
<summary>How Auto chooses a printer</summary>

There are two kinds of template. [Thermal templates](../../receipts/thermal-templates.mdx) are laid out for receipt printers, a fixed number of characters to a line. [HTML templates](../../receipts/html-templates.mdx) are laid out like a web page, an A4 invoice for example.

- A thermal template goes to a receipt printer whose paper width suits it: a 58 mm template to a printer set to 32 characters, an 80 mm one to 42 or 48.
- An HTML template goes to **Print Dialog**.
- If several printers suit, the default wins.

When the printer chosen for a template doesn't suit it, an amber warning appears on the receipt screen, and it still prints: a thermal template sent to **Print Dialog** prints its on-screen preview, and an HTML template sent to a receipt printer prints the built-in receipt layout. The one exception is a cloud printer that only takes thermal templates: it prints nothing for an HTML one, and the warning says so.

</details>

:::note Printing every receipt automatically
Printing when a sale completes is a cart setting, not a printer one. Open the settings in the cart header and turn on **Automatically print receipt after checkout**. Routing then decides which printer it uses.
:::

## If something goes wrong {#when-nothing-prints}

**The scan finds nothing.** Check the printer is on and has paper. On Wi-Fi, give it a fixed address and use **Enter an IP address**: see [Give the printer a fixed address](./network-address.mdx). In the browser, allow local network access: see [Browser](./browser-permissions.mdx).

**Test Print says it failed.** Read the line in the message or the window. Then press **Copy setup report** on the row, or **Support details** in **Edit Printer**, and send it with your support request. It says what the app tried.

**The drawer doesn't open.** Press **Open drawer** in **Edit Printer**. If nothing happens, switch **Cash drawer connector** to the other pin, and match **Printer Language** to the printer's emulation. See [Connecting a cash drawer](./cash-drawer.mdx).

**The receipt prints, but not the way it looks on screen.** Look for the amber warning on the receipt screen and pick the printer the template is made for.

Pick the guide that matches:

**Setting up**

- [Getting the receipt width right](./receipt-width.mdx) — the ruler overshoots or falls short
- [Give the printer a fixed address](./network-address.mdx) — nothing found on Wi-Fi, or a printer that stopped working
- [Pairing a Bluetooth receipt printer](./bluetooth-pairing.mdx) — it isn't in the chooser
- [USB on Windows](./usb-windows.mdx) and [USB on Linux](./usb-linux.mdx)
- [Supported receipt printers](./supported-printers.mdx)

**Epson prints nothing**

- [Epson Secure Printing](./secure-printing.mdx) — a new Epson that appears to send and prints nothing
- [Server Direct Print](./server-direct-print.mdx) — its web page works but no job prints
- [Printed once and then stopped](./printed-once-then-stopped.mdx) — power-cycle first

**Permissions**

- [iPad and iPhone](./ios-local-network.mdx)
- [Android](./android-permissions.mdx)
- [Browser](./browser-permissions.mdx)

**Something on the receipt is wrong**

- [The logo is missing in the browser](./logo-missing-browser.mdx)
- [Receipts in Chinese, Thai, Arabic or Cyrillic](./non-latin-receipts.mdx)

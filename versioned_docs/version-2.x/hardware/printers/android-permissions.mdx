**Symptom:** on an Android phone or tablet, **Add printer** says **No printer found**, or your Bluetooth printer is missing from the results.

If WCPOS isn't allowed to use Bluetooth, it can't find Bluetooth printers. Check the permission first.

## Let WCPOS reach your printer {#allow-nearby-devices}

1. Check Bluetooth is on in Android's quick settings.
2. Find your Android version: open Android **Settings → About phone → Android version**.
3. Open Android **Settings → Apps → WCPOS → Permissions**.
4. Allow the permission for your version:
   - **Android 12 to 16:** tap **Nearby devices** and choose **Allow**. This lets WCPOS find Bluetooth printers and connect to them.
   - **Android 17 and newer:** tap **Nearby devices** and choose **Allow**. The same switch also lets WCPOS reach printers on your Wi-Fi.
   - **Android 11 and older:** there is no Nearby devices permission. Tap **Location** and allow it. These versions need Location to find Bluetooth printers.
5. Go back to WCPOS and tap **Scan again** on the **Add printer** screen.

Menu names in Android's own Settings vary a little between phone makers, but every Android phone has a **Permissions** page for each app.

## Bluetooth printers {#bluetooth-printers}

Once the permission is allowed, WCPOS looks for Bluetooth printers. Whether it finds yours also depends on the kind of Bluetooth the printer uses:

- **Bluetooth Low Energy printers** show up in the scan by themselves. They don't need pairing in Android.
- **Classic Bluetooth printers**, which include many low-cost 58 mm printers, only show up once Android has paired them. On the **No printer found** screen, tap **Open Bluetooth settings**, pair the printer there, then come back and tap **Scan again**.

Not sure which kind you have, or the printer won't pair? [Pairing a Bluetooth receipt printer](./bluetooth-pairing.mdx#classic-or-low-energy) explains how to tell, and how to put the printer into pairing mode.

## Wi-Fi printers {#wi-fi-printers}

Check the phone or tablet is on the shop's Wi-Fi, the same network as the printer, and not on mobile data or a guest network.

If the scan still comes back empty but you know the printer's address, tap **Enter an IP address**. It skips the scan and checks that one address directly. On Android 17 and newer, Nearby devices covers Wi-Fi too, so [allow it](#allow-nearby-devices) first. The address is printed on the printer's status sheet. See [Finding your printer's network address](./network-address.mdx).

## If something goes wrong {#goes-wrong}

**A red line under No printer found says printer discovery failed, for example "Bluetooth is off or not allowed for this app."** Bluetooth is off on the phone, or WCPOS isn't allowed to use it. Turn Bluetooth on and [allow the permission for your Android version](#allow-nearby-devices), then tap **Scan again**.

**"The app was not allowed to reach the printer. Allow it in your system settings, then try again."** Android refused WCPOS when it tried to print. [Allow the permission for your Android version](#allow-nearby-devices), then tap **Try again**.

**"Pair the printer in this phone's Bluetooth settings, then scan again."** The printer uses Classic Bluetooth and Android no longer has it paired. Tap **Open Bluetooth settings** below the message, pair it, then tap **Start over** to scan again.

**Nearby devices (or Location on Android 11 and older) is allowed and the printer still isn't found.** Turn the printer off and on, then tap **Scan again**. If it is still missing, see [Pairing a Bluetooth receipt printer](./bluetooth-pairing.mdx) for the other causes.

**Still stuck?** After **Nothing came out**, tap **Copy setup report**. On Android it opens the share menu: send the report to us by email, or copy it into your message. It lists your app version, the printer, what the app tried and what the printer answered.

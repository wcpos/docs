The app has its own setup wizard: **Add printer** looks for your printer, prints a test page, and asks whether it came out right. This page walks through it. If the test page doesn't print, [answer a few questions](#troubleshoot). The printer's settings, and how to change them later, are [at the end](#more-options).

## Add a printer {#add-a-printer}

Switch the printer on and load paper first: the search starts the moment the panel opens. Then go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Add printer**. Under **Printers** you'll already see **Print Dialog**, the built-in printer every till has.

## What the search finds {#what-it-finds}

Each printer it finds is a card. The heading says how it went: **Found your printer**, **Which printer is yours?** when there are several, **That is an office printer, not a receipt printer**, or **No printer found**.

If your printer's card shows **Ready** or **Test needed**, go on to [Print a test page](#test-page).

A card carries one of four badges:

- **Ready** — the app can print to it. Press **Print a test page**.
- **Test needed** — the app thinks it can print, but only a test page will tell.
- **Office printer** — an office or inkjet printer, not a receipt printer. Its card can't be picked or tested. Receipts print on it through the built-in **Print Dialog** instead.
- **No connection** — nothing at that address answered as a printer the app can reach.

If the search finds nothing, see [No printer found](#not-found).

## Print a test page {#test-page}

{/* reshoot: needs a real printer on the network (see reshoot.md) */}

Pick your printer's card and press **Print a test page** (or **Print a test page on** and its name, when there are several). While the app checks the paper, the panel says **Checking paper width…**. If it can't tell, **Paper width** lets you pick **58 mm** or **80 mm** first.

Then the panel asks **Did the test page print?** Under the question, **Test page** *n* **·** *n* **characters per line** shows the width in use. Before you answer, look at the **COLUMN RULER** on the test page: a line of digits that must end flush right. If it wraps onto a second line or stops short of the edge, the width is off. Then pick the answer that matches.

### Yes, and it looks right {#test-page-ok}

The printer is saved. The panel says *name* **is set up**, then **It is your default printer.** or **Receipts will print here.** Press **Done**.

### Printed, but the ruler is off {#test-page-ruler}

Under **Which paper is in the printer?**, pick the width and the test page prints again:

- **32 — 58 mm paper**
- **42 — 80 mm, older printers**
- **48 — 80 mm, most Epson & Star**
- **64 — 80 mm, small font**

See [receipt width](./receipt-width.mdx).

### Nothing came out {#test-page-nothing}

The panel shows **Nothing printed** and one line on the likely cause, such as paper or Secure Printing. Fix it, then:

- **Try again** — prints the test page again.
- **Save without testing** — saves the printer anyway. Use it when the printer is off or out of paper right now and you know its settings are right. Test it later from its row with **Test Print**.
- **Start over**, **Copy setup report** (for support) and **Open the printer guide**.

Still nothing? [Answer a few questions](#troubleshoot).

### Enter the address yourself {#enter-address}

If the search missed a Wi-Fi or network printer, press **Enter an IP address**. The address is on the printer's status sheet. On most Epson printers, hold **Feed** while you switch it on to print it.

  <WizardStep id="network-setup" title="Add a network printer">

Go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Add printer**. The app looks for printers on your network. If yours isn't found, press **Enter an IP address**, type the address and press **Check this address**. Then pick its card and press **Print a test page**.

To find the IP address, print the printer's network status sheet:

- **Star:** hold the **Feed** button for about 5 seconds while the printer is already on.
- **Epson:** hold the **Feed** button while you switch the printer on.

  <WizardFix id="fix-not-printing" title="When the test page doesn't come out">

Read the line under **Nothing printed** first: it names the likely cause, such as Secure Printing, held jobs or paper.

Then the quick checks: is there paper, is the cover closed, and is the paper loaded the right way? Thermal paper prints on one side only. If it's in upside-down the printer feeds blank paper. Scratch the paper with a fingernail: the side that marks must face the print head.

Then confirm the printer is reachable: open `http://<printer-ip>/` in a web browser (if that doesn't load, try `https://<printer-ip>/`). If you see the printer's own settings page, it's on the network and the problem is in the settings. If neither loads, it's a network problem: a wrong address, or the printer isn't connected.

  <WizardFix id="net-deep" title="Deeper network checks">
If the basics are right and it still won't print, one of these is usually the cause:

**Wrong address, or it changed.** Check the address on the status sheet matches the one in the app. It's easy to use another printer's address. Printers on DHCP can change address when they restart; set a **DHCP reservation** on your router. An address starting `169.254` means the printer never joined the network. See [fixed address](./network-address.mdx).

**Epson printed once, then stopped.** Switch the printer off and on again. See [printed once then stopped](./printed-once-then-stopped.mdx).

**Epson Secure Printing is on.** The printer refuses plain jobs. See [Secure Printing](./secure-printing.mdx).

**Epson prints nothing and its web page still works.** Server Direct Print may be holding it. See [Server Direct Print](./server-direct-print.mdx).

**Two tills, one printer.** Many Star network printers accept one connection at a time by default, so the second till times out. Turn on **9100 multi-session** in the printer's web settings.

**The printer only accepts other protocols.** The desktop and mobile apps print over **raw TCP on port 9100**. Open the printer's settings page in a browser and make sure **raw / port 9100** printing is on. A printer set to LPR, IPP or WSD only never receives the job.

**Wi-Fi won't join, or keeps dropping.** Receipt printers are usually **2.4 GHz only**. Put them on a 2.4 GHz network, not a 5 GHz-only one. For a fixed till, a network cable is the reliable fix.

**The first print of the day fails.** The printer's network card went to sleep and the first job woke it. Print again. For a fixed till, a network cable is the reliable fix.

**iPhone, iPad or Android can't see the printer.** The app needs permission to reach your local network. See [iPad and iPhone](./ios-local-network.mdx) or [Android permissions](./android-permissions.mdx).

  <WizardStep id="web-net-setup" title="Add a network printer in the web app">

From a **web browser**, the app can only talk to the printer's built-in web server over HTTP or HTTPS. On an **HTTPS** store it uses the printer's secure port by default (**8043** for Epson, **443** for Star), which only works if the printer has a certificate your browser trusts.

First, confirm the printer is reachable: open `http://<printer-ip>/` in a new browser tab (if that doesn't load, try `https://<printer-ip>/`). If you see the printer's own settings page, it's on the network. If neither loads, fix the network or address first.

Then go to **Settings → Printing**, press **Add printer**, and let it search **Wi-Fi**. If yours isn't found, press **Enter an IP address**. Chrome may ask for permission to reach your local network: allow it.

  <WizardFix id="web-net-fix" title="When web network printing won't work">

This is the most common dead end, and it often can't be fixed from the browser. Here's why, and what to do:

Print the printer's **status sheet** and look at the SSL/TLS section. If there's no certificate, HTTPS printing can't work until you create one. A browser won't trust the printer's own certificate until you open `https://<printer-ip>` once and accept it, on every device. See [permissions and certificates](./browser-permissions.mdx).

If the job is sent but nothing prints, the browser probably blocked it: the printer speaks HTTP but your store is HTTPS (a "mixed content" block), or the secure port has no certificate behind it.

The web app reaches a printer only through its **built-in web server**, Epson's **ePOS-Print** or Star's **WebPRNT**, so make sure that service is on in the printer's settings. Only Epson "intelligent" and **-NT** models and Star **WebPRNT** models have one. If yours doesn't, a browser can't reach it.

**The reliable fix is the desktop app**, which prints straight to the printer on port 9100 with none of the browser's HTTPS limits. Add the same printer there.

  <WizardStep id="bt-setup" title="Connect over Bluetooth">

There are two kinds of Bluetooth printer. **Bluetooth Low Energy** printers, most small 58 mm ones, are picked in the app: don't pair them in your system settings. **Bluetooth Classic** printers, most Epson and Star models, are paired in the system's Bluetooth settings first. On iPhone and iPad, Bluetooth Classic works for Epson and Star only. On Android, other Classic printers work once paired. See [Bluetooth pairing](./bluetooth-pairing.mdx).

A Bluetooth printer talks to one device at a time. If it's still connected to another till, disconnect it there first. On **iPhone or iPad**, allow the app **Bluetooth** and **Local Network** when it asks. On **Android**, allow **Nearby devices**; see [Android permissions](./android-permissions.mdx).

Then go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Add printer**. In the desktop app, press **Find a Bluetooth printer** and pick yours under **Choose your Bluetooth printer**. On iPhone, iPad and Android, the search looks for Bluetooth printers by itself: pick your printer's card. Then press **Print a test page**.

If the printer isn't in the list, it's probably still connected to another device, or it's Bluetooth Classic and not yet paired in the system settings. Disconnect it from the other device (or switch that device's Bluetooth off), switch the printer off and on, then scan again. Check it's on and in range. On iPhone and iPad, a Bluetooth Classic printer that isn't Epson or Star won't connect: use Bluetooth Low Energy, Wi-Fi or USB instead. On Android, set the app's battery use to **Unrestricted** so the system doesn't drop the connection. See [Bluetooth pairing](./bluetooth-pairing.mdx).

Connecting a printer to the **web app** over Bluetooth or USB works only in **Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge**. Safari and Firefox can't do it. Go to **Settings → Printing**, press **Add printer**, then **Find a USB printer** or **Find a Bluetooth printer**, and choose the printer in the browser's chooser.

The printer is being sent commands it doesn't understand.

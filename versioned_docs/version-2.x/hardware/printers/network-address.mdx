**Symptom:** the search for a Wi-Fi printer ends on **No printer found**. Or a printer that worked for weeks stops, and every receipt fails with **The selected printer cannot be reached** ([PRINT301](../../error-codes/PRINT301.mdx)).

The printer is usually fine. The till can't reach it, either because the two are on different networks or because the printer's address changed.

- **Adding a printer?** Check the Wi-Fi first, then [add it by its address](#enter-address).
- **A printer that worked has stopped?** Read its address, then [change it on the printer you have](#change-address).

## Check the Wi-Fi first {#same-wifi}

Put the till and the printer on the **same Wi-Fi**, not the guest network. Then go to **Settings → Printing**, press **Add printer**, and let the search run. If the printer shows up, add it and [make its address stay put](#fixed-address). If not, read on.

## Why it happens {#why-it-happens}

- **Different networks.** The till is on one Wi-Fi and the printer on another: a guest network, a second router, or a separate network for staff devices. The search only looks on the till's own network.
- **Guest Wi-Fi keeps devices apart.** Guest and public Wi-Fi usually stop the devices on it from talking to each other. The printer is on the same Wi-Fi and still can't be reached.
- **The address moved.** Your router lends each device an address for a while. After a restart, or when the loan runs out, the printer can get a new one while the app still prints to the old one. It worked yesterday and fails today.

<details>
<summary>In a web browser, the search only tries common addresses</summary>

A browser can't ask the network what is on it, so the search tries the address ranges most routers use: `192.168.0.x`, `192.168.1.x`, `192.168.4.x`, `192.168.10.x`, `10.0.0.x`, `10.0.1.x` and `172.16.0.x`. A printer on any other range is never found by the search. Add it by its address instead. In a browser the app reaches Epson and Star printers over Wi-Fi; see [what each app can connect to](./index.mdx#supported-printers-by-platform).

</details>

## Read the printer's address {#read-the-address}

The printer prints its own address on its **status sheet**. Some makers call it the network self-test or configuration page.

- **Epson:** with the printer on, open the roll paper cover, hold **Feed** for at least a second, then close the cover. A model with a status sheet button on the back prints it when you hold that button for about three seconds.
- **Star:** with the printer on, hold **Feed** for about five seconds.

Find the **IP address** line on the sheet, for example `192.168.1.100`. Two printers in the shop? Check you have the sheet from the right one.

## Make the address stay put {#fixed-address}

Give the printer a fixed address, so you only enter it once. Ask whoever looks after the shop's router to **reserve the printer's address** for it: the address on its status sheet. Then the router gives the printer that address every time.

<details>
<summary>Router and printer settings</summary>

- **Reserve it on the router (best).** In your router's settings, open the page that lists the devices on your network (often called DHCP or LAN). Find the printer by the address on its status sheet, and reserve that address for it. Nothing changes on the printer.
- **Or set it on the printer.** Open the printer's address in a web browser on the same network, for example `http://192.168.1.100`, and give it a fixed (static) address in its network settings. Pick one your router doesn't hand out to other devices, or two devices can end up with the same address. The router's settings show which addresses it hands out.

</details>

## Add the printer by its address {#enter-address}

The search finds a printer by asking the network. Typing the address skips the asking, so it works when the search can't.

Go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Add printer**. The search starts by itself. Press **Enter an IP address**, while the search runs or after it ends. It stops the search.

## Printer already set up? Change its address {#change-address}

When a printer that worked stops because its address moved, change the address on the printer you have. You don't need to add it again.

1. Go to **Settings → Printing**.
2. Press **Edit** on the printer's row.
3. Change **IP Address** to the address on the status sheet. The change saves as you type. **Scan Network** looks for the printer instead.
4. Press **Test Print**.

## If something goes wrong {#if-something-goes-wrong}

- **The address on the sheet starts with `169.254`.** The printer never got an address from the network. Check its network cable or its Wi-Fi settings, then print the status sheet again.
- **No connection, and the address is right.** The till and the printer are on different networks, or guest Wi-Fi keeps them apart. See [Why it happens](#why-it-happens).
- **On an iPad or iPhone, the search finds nothing.** The app needs **Local Network** permission. See [Let the app find printers on your iPad or iPhone](./ios-local-network.mdx).
- **In a browser, the search finds nothing, or the desktop app prints and the browser doesn't.** The browser needs permission to reach your network. See [Printing from the browser](./browser-permissions.mdx).
- **The address is right, and it printed once and then stopped.** The printer may be holding a job. See [Epson printed once and then stopped](./printed-once-then-stopped.mdx).

## Still stuck? {#still-stuck}

Copy the setup report and paste it into your message. It's on the **Nothing printed** screen of **Add printer**, and in the **⋯** menu on the printer's row in **Settings → Printing**: press **Copy setup report**. It holds the address and port the app used, what the printer's ports answered, and the app's last printer log lines. Nothing leaves the device until you paste it.

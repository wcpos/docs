**The symptom:** the receipt looks right on screen, but the paper shows question marks, empty boxes or the wrong letters.

This page is about receipt printers. The **Print Dialog** and office printers use your computer's fonts, so they print every language as it looks on screen.

## Choose a fix {#choose-a-fix}

First, see what your printer offers: go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Edit** on the printer's row.

<details>
<summary>No Edit button? The printer is marked managed by WCPOS</summary>

A printer marked **managed by WCPOS** is a cloud printer. It has no **Edit**, because it is set up in **WP Admin → POS → Settings → Cloud Print** by whoever runs your store, not in the app. How cloud printers print is on [Cloud Printing](../../receipts/cloud-printing.mdx).

</details>

**Fast, prints as text: [set the receipt language](#receipt-language)** when **Edit printer** shows **Receipt language** and the receipt is in Cyrillic, Greek, Turkish, Vietnamese, or accented Western or Central European letters.

**Slower, works for every language: [print receipts as an image](#print-the-receipt-as-an-image)** when:

- **Edit printer** has no **Receipt language**.
- The receipt is in Chinese, Japanese or Korean.
- The receipt is in Arabic, Hebrew or Thai. These have a receipt language, but print best as an image. Try the receipt language first only if speed matters.
- One receipt mixes two alphabets, such as Greek and Russian.
- A receipt language is set and the letters are still wrong.

<details>
<summary>Why some printers have no Receipt language</summary>

**Receipt language** shows only when **Printer Language** is **ESC/POS**, the command language of Epson and most other receipt printers (see [Supported Printers](supported-printers.mdx)). A Star printer set to **StarPRNT** or **Star Line Mode** has none: for non-Latin text, turn on [Print receipts as an image](#print-the-receipt-as-an-image).

</details>

## Set the receipt language {#receipt-language}

Choose the language your receipts are printed in. The receipt still prints as text, so it stays fast.

**For a printer you already have:**

1. Go to **Settings → Printing** and press **Edit** on the printer's row.
2. Open **Receipt language** and choose the entry named for your receipt's language. **Edit printer** saves each change as you make it.
3. Press **Test Print** at the bottom of **Edit printer**. If the letters are still wrong, your printer may not have that language built in: [print as an image](#print-the-receipt-as-an-image) instead.

1. **Printer Language** — **ESC/POS** here. **Receipt language** shows only for ESC/POS.
2. **Paper width** — the characters per line. See [Receipt Width](receipt-width.mdx).
3. **Receipt language** — open it and choose the entry for your receipt's language. Every printer starts on **Automatic**.
4. **Print receipts as an image** — the other fix, for every language. See [Print receipts as an image](#print-the-receipt-as-an-image).

</JourneyShot>

**While adding a printer:** in the **Add printer** panel, press **More options**. **Receipt language** is the fourth setting, under **Printer text width** (the same setting **Edit printer** calls **Paper width**).

The list has eleven entries. Chinese, Japanese and Korean have none: [print those as an image](#print-the-receipt-as-an-image). Some entries are named for a region or an alphabet, not a language: Russian, Ukrainian and Bulgarian are under **Cyrillic**, and Polish, Czech and Hungarian under **Central European**.

<details>
<summary>Which receipt language should I choose?</summary>

| Entry | For |
|---|---|
| **Automatic** | The app picks a table line by line. Where every printer starts |
| **English** | Plain English text |
| **Western European** | French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch and the Nordic languages |
| **Central European** | Polish, Czech, Slovak, Hungarian, Croatian, Slovenian |
| **Cyrillic** | Russian, Ukrainian, Bulgarian, Serbian |
| **Greek**, **Turkish**, **Arabic**, **Hebrew**, **Thai**, **Vietnamese** | That language |

</details>

**Receipt language** shows only when **Printer Language** in **Edit printer** is **ESC/POS**, which is what Epson and most other receipt printers use.

<details>
<summary>The other settings in More options</summary>

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={1} offsets={[[100, -18], [463, 0], [0, 0], [442, -38], [442, 45], [-34, 0], [-34, 0]]} alt="More options open in the Add printer panel: Printer Name, Vendor with Epson and Star Micronics, Printer text width, Receipt language set to Automatic, Auto-cut paper and Auto-open cash drawer">

1. **More options** — opens the printer's settings in the panel, before the test page. Press it again to close them.
2. **Printer Name** — the name on the printer's row in **Settings → Printing** and in the printer lists. **Receipt Printer** until you change it.
3. **Vendor** — **Epson** or **Star Micronics**. Choosing **Star Micronics** hides **Receipt language**: on a Star printer, [print non-Latin text as an image](#print-the-receipt-as-an-image).
4. **Printer text width** — how many characters fit on one line: 32 for 58 mm paper, 42, 48 or 64 for 80 mm. The test page checks it. See [Receipt Width](receipt-width.mdx).
5. **Receipt language** — the character table for the receipt's text. **Automatic** lets the app choose line by line. The list is [above](#receipt-language).
6. **Auto-cut paper** — cuts the paper after each receipt.
7. **Auto-open cash drawer** — opens a drawer plugged into this printer. See [Cash Drawer](cash-drawer.mdx).

</JourneyShot>

Below these, **Set as default** makes this the printer for any receipt that isn't sent somewhere else. Everything here can be changed later in **Edit printer**. The rest of the panel is on [Setup Wizard](setup-wizard.mdx).

</details>

## Print receipts as an image {#print-the-receipt-as-an-image}

This fix works for every language, on any receipt printer you added in the app. The app draws the receipt as a picture and sends the picture, so the printer's character tables no longer matter.

:::warning
On an Epson connected by Wi-Fi or network cable, printing as an image needs [Secure Printing](secure-printing.mdx) turned off.
:::

1. Go to **Settings → Printing**.
2. Press **Edit** on the printer's row.
3. Turn on **Print receipts as an image**, the last switch in **Edit printer**. Its note reads: *For scripts the printer cannot render (Arabic, Hebrew, Thai). Slower and larger than text.*
4. Press **Test Print** at the bottom of **Edit printer** to check. The printer's row in **Settings → Printing** has **Test Print** too.

<JourneyShot manifest={manifest} device="tablet" step={4} offsets={[[680, 0], [680, 0], [680, 0], [680, 0]]} maxWidth={799} alt="The settings in Edit printer, with Print receipts as an image the last switch, off, and its note: For scripts the printer cannot render (Arabic, Hebrew, Thai). Slower and larger than text.">

1. **Printer Language** — **ESC/POS** here. The image works on Star printers too, which have no **Receipt language**.
2. **Paper width** — the characters per line. See [Receipt Width](receipt-width.mdx).
3. **Receipt language** — an image doesn't use the printer's character tables, so this no longer matters.
4. **Print receipts as an image** — the switch, off here. Turn it on, then press **Test Print** to check.

</JourneyShot>

The switch is in **Edit printer** only, not in **More options**: add the printer first, then turn it on.

:::tip
An image is much more to send than text, so each receipt takes longer to print. Turn it on only for the printers that need it.
:::

## Why it happens {#why-it-happens}

A receipt printer doesn't use your computer's fonts. When it has no way to print your letters as text, they come out as question marks or boxes; an image avoids that.

<details>
<summary>How a receipt printer prints text</summary>

It prints text from character tables built into the printer, one table at a time. For each line, the app picks a table that holds its letters. That works for English and most European receipts. For other scripts the printer may not have a table the app can pick, and every letter it can't print comes out as a question mark or a box. **Receipt language** fixes the table for the whole receipt; an image skips the tables altogether.

</details>

## Still stuck? {#still-stuck}

On the printer's row in **Settings → Printing**, open the **⋯** menu and press **Copy setup report**. In the Android and iOS apps this opens the share sheet instead. Paste the report into your message, with a photo of the printed receipt and the language you're printing.

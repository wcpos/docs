## Highest-leverage findings for the page under review

1. **When the till refuses a coupon: structure and task clarity.** The till shows a red message (for example, "This coupon has expired."). The 14 collapsed causes below it are titled with paraphrases like "Coupon used up" and "Customer has used their share", not with that message. The page says each one "gives the till's exact message", but the cashier only sees it after opening the right one. On a phone that means opening sections one by one to match the words on screen. **Smallest fix:** start each section title with the till's exact red text, then the short name. For example: *"This coupon has expired." – Expired coupon*.

2. **When the till refuses a coupon, screenshot: visuals.** This shot has only two labels, and neither is on the most important thing in it: the red line "This coupon has expired." Label 1 points at the coupon field, and the cashier is told to look "above it". **Cancel**, the other way out named in the text, has no label either. **Smallest fix:** add a marker on the red line ("The reason. Find it in the list below") and one on **Cancel**, then renumber the legend to 1–4.

3. **Apply it, screenshot at phone width: visuals.** The shot is the whole till scaled down to phone width. Markers 2, 3 and 4 sit on top of each other over totals that can't be read. The struck-through line total, which the text describes just above, has no label. **Smallest fix:** crop the shot to the cart column, which makes the rows readable on a phone and separates the markers. Add one marker on the line's struck-through $45.00 → $36.00 ("each line shows its share of the coupon").
